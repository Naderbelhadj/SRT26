\paragraph{Background.}
Diabetic retinopathy (DR) affects about 103 million adults and is a leading cause of
preventable blindness. None of the representative systems reviewed here addresses three
requirements: L1 (relational context between patients), L2 (global vascular topology),
and L3 (safety-oriented grading: grade order and missed referrals of severe cases are
rarely modelled or measured). ``Disease progression'' is operationalised as
cross-sectional prediction of severity on the ordinal International Clinical DR (ICDR)
scale from a single fundus image, not as
longitudinal prediction.

\paragraph{Methods.}
Twelve publications support this thesis. Three hypotheses structure it: H1, a population
graph adds information beyond that captured by a per-image classifier; H2,
$H_0/H_1$ persistent homology of the retinal vasculature is associated with severity; H3,
an ordinal pipeline meets screening safety criteria (quadratic weighted kappa, QWK,
$\geq0.80$; severe and proliferative non-referral rates $\leq2\%$; no
Grade~$0\leftrightarrow4$ confusion)
internally and on external data (H3a), while none of its reduced variants does (H3b). The
systems are TOPORET, a topology-aware population graph evaluated transductively, and two models trained on $5{,}859$
fused images: DGTS (graph regularised by persistent homology) and DOTS (CORAL ordinal head).

\paragraph{Results.}
H1 is supported ($t_4=3.82$, two-tailed $p=0.019$ over five folds; $+0.04$ QWK), and for
three benchmarks combined after correction for fold overlap ($p=0.015$). H2 is supported as a statistical association
($F=84.3$, $p<0.001$). DGTS reaches QWK $=0.941$ in five-fold cross-validation and $0.925$
without retraining on $35{,}126$ EyePACS images. DOTS reaches QWK $=0.953$ with severe
non-referral (Sev-NR) $0.97\%$ internally, and QWK $=0.931$ with Sev-NR $1.4\%$ on the
independent Messidor-2 dataset, where none of four reduced variants reaches Sev-NR
$\leq2\%$ (H3a and H3b supported at the point estimate). With $309$ severe images, the
internal Sev-NR interval ($[0.2\%,\,2.8\%]$) still extends above $2\%$.

\paragraph{Conclusions.}
The systems are not claimed to be clinically validated or regulatory-ready, and the three
axes are demonstrated in complementary systems rather than in one model. The thesis shows,
on retrospective public data, that relational, topological and ordinal-safety mechanisms
each bring a measurable, externally validated benefit. This is Level~1 (computational,
retrospective) evidence; a pathway towards a unified model and prospective validation is
outlined.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} diabetic retinopathy; graph neural networks; GraphSAGE;
persistent homology; ordinal regression; CORAL; external validation; safety-oriented evaluation;
medical device software.
