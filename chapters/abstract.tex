\paragraph{Background.}
Diabetic retinopathy (DR) affects about 103 million adults and is a leading cause of
preventable blindness. Representative grading systems share three
limitations: they grade each image in isolation (L1), ignore the global topology of the
retinal vessels (L2), and rarely model the order of the grades or measure missed referrals
of severe cases (L3). The task is the cross-sectional prediction of the five-grade ICDR
severity from a single fundus image.

\paragraph{Methods.}
Three hypotheses are tested. H1: a population graph adds information beyond a per-image
classifier. H2: $H_0/H_1$ persistent homology of the vessel network is associated with
severity. H3: an ordinal pipeline satisfies pre-set screening-oriented targets (quadratic weighted kappa,
QWK, $\geq0.80$; severe and proliferative non-referral rates $\leq2\%$; no
Grade~$0\leftrightarrow4$ confusion) internally and on external data (H3a), whereas none of
its reduced variants does (H3b). Three systems are developed: TOPORET, a topology-aware
population graph evaluated transductively on three benchmarks; and, on a fused corpus of
$5{,}859$ images, DGTS, a population graph regularised by persistent homology, and DOTS, an
ordinal pipeline with a CORAL head; nine accepted papers support the work.

\paragraph{Results.}
The five-fold pre-specified test provides evidence supporting H1 ($t_4=3.82$, two-tailed $p=0.019$,
$+0.04$ QWK); corrected for fold overlap, it is significant only for the three benchmarks
combined ($p=0.015$). H2 holds as a small statistical association, not
a causal or biological link ($F=84.3$, $p<0.001$, $\eta^2\approx0.01$). DGTS reaches QWK $=0.941$ in cross-validation and $0.925$
without retraining on $35{,}126$ EyePACS images. DOTS reaches QWK $=0.953$ and a severe
non-referral rate (Sev-NR) of $0.97\%$ internally, and QWK $=0.931$ with Sev-NR $=1.4\%$ on
the independent Messidor-2 dataset, where none of four reduced variants reaches Sev-NR
$\leq2\%$: the observed performance satisfies the pre-set targets (H3a, H3b) at the
point estimate, but the internal Sev-NR interval ($309$ severe images, $[0.2\%,\,2.8\%]$)
still exceeds $2\%$.

\paragraph{Conclusions.}
TOPORET, DGTS and DOTS form a three-axis research framework, not a single model, for one
objective: exploiting relations, topology and ordinality in DR grading. The findings are
retrospective evidence that these mechanisms contribute complementary information; they
support further clinical validation but establish neither prospective clinical safety nor
regulatory compliance.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} diabetic retinopathy; graph neural networks; GraphSAGE;
persistent homology; ordinal regression; CORAL; external validation; safety-oriented evaluation;
