\paragraph{Background.}
Diabetic retinopathy (DR) affects about 103 million adults and is a leading cause of
preventable blindness. The representative grading systems reviewed in this thesis share
three limitations: they grade each image in isolation (L1), ignore the global topology of
the retinal vessels (L2), and rarely model the order of the grades or measure missed
referrals of severe cases (L3). ``Disease progression'' is operationalised as the
cross-sectional prediction of severity on the five-grade International Clinical DR (ICDR)
scale from a single fundus image, not as longitudinal prediction.

\paragraph{Methods.}
Three hypotheses are tested. H1: a population graph adds information beyond a per-image
classifier. H2: $H_0/H_1$ persistent homology of the vessel network is associated with
severity. H3: an ordinal pipeline meets screening safety criteria (quadratic weighted kappa,
QWK, $\geq0.80$; severe and proliferative non-referral rates $\leq2\%$; no
Grade~$0\leftrightarrow4$ confusion) internally and on external data (H3a), whereas none of
its reduced variants does (H3b). Three systems are developed: TOPORET, a topology-aware
population graph evaluated transductively on three benchmarks; and, on a fused corpus of
$5{,}859$ images, DGTS, a population graph regularised by persistent homology, and DOTS, an
ordinal pipeline with a CORAL head; twelve publications support the work.

\paragraph{Results.}
H1 is supported by the pre-specified test ($t_4=3.82$, two-tailed $p=0.019$, $+0.04$ QWK)
and, after correction for the overlap of cross-validation folds, by the three benchmarks
combined ($p=0.015$). H2 is supported as a small but robust association ($F=84.3$,
$p<0.001$, $\eta^2\approx0.01$). DGTS reaches QWK $=0.941$ in cross-validation and $0.925$
without retraining on $35{,}126$ EyePACS images. DOTS reaches QWK $=0.953$ and a severe
non-referral rate (Sev-NR) of $0.97\%$ internally, and QWK $=0.931$ with Sev-NR $=1.4\%$ on
the independent Messidor-2 dataset, where none of four reduced variants reaches Sev-NR
$\leq2\%$: H3a and H3b hold at the point estimate, but the internal Sev-NR interval
($309$ severe images, $[0.2\%,\,2.8\%]$) still exceeds $2\%$.

\paragraph{Conclusions.}
On retrospective public data, relational, topological and ordinal-safety mechanisms each
bring a measurable, externally validated benefit to DR grading. This is Level~1
(computational, retrospective) evidence, obtained in complementary systems rather than one
model, and not a clinical validation; a path towards a unified model and prospective
validation is outlined.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} diabetic retinopathy; graph neural networks; GraphSAGE;
persistent homology; ordinal regression; CORAL; external validation; safety-oriented evaluation;
medical device software.
