\paragraph{Background.}
Diabetic retinopathy (DR) affects about 103 million adults and is a leading cause of
preventable blindness. None of the representative systems reviewed here addresses, in one evaluated framework,
three requirements: L1 (relational context between patients), L2 (global vascular
topology), and L3 (a safety mechanism: every image is graded, and the Severe-DR
Non-Referral Rate, Sev-NR, stays above the $2\%$ target adopted here). ``Disease
progression'' is operationalised as cross-sectional prediction of ordinal ICDR severity
from a single fundus image, not as longitudinal prediction.

\paragraph{Methods.}
Eleven publications support this thesis. Three hypotheses are pre-specified, each with
its test and rejection threshold: H1, an inductive population graph adds
information beyond that captured by a per-image classifier; H2, $H_0/H_1$ persistent homology
of the retinal vasculature is associated with severity; H3, the combination of the three
axes reaches seven predefined computational criteria (H3a) and, within the investigated
architectural family, no proper subset of the axes does (H3b). A graph template first evaluated on
distributed systems is carried over, by analogy, to hybrid models, the TOPORET population
graph and the DOTS system.

\paragraph{Results.}
H1 is supported ($t_4=3.82$, two-tailed $p=0.019$ over five patient-aware splits;
$+0.04$ quadratic weighted kappa, QWK). H2 is supported as a statistical association
($F=84.3$, $p<0.001$). On public benchmarks ($108{,}301$ images,
not necessarily independent patients; $10{,}000$ held-out test images), DOTS reaches QWK $=0.951$, AUC $=0.988$, ECE $=0.040$,
Sev-NR $=1.9\%$, coverage $93.8\%$ and $0\%$ ordinal rank violations. The Sev-NR 95\%
confidence interval, $[1.4\%,\,2.4\%]$, extends above the $2\%$ target: H3a is met at the
point estimate only, not with statistical confidence. Within the investigated family, all
six proper subsets of the axes miss Sev-NR $<2\%$ (H3b).

\paragraph{Conclusions.}
DOTS is not claimed to be clinically validated or regulatory-ready; the thesis shows that, within the investigated architectural family, relational, structural/topological and
ordinal-safety mechanisms can be integrated into a single system that achieves favourable
safety-oriented performance on retrospective public benchmarks. This is Level~1
(computational, retrospective) evidence, not prospective clinical safety; a planned
pathway towards prospective validation and regulatory assessment is outlined.
