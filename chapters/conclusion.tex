\chapter*{General Conclusion}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{General Conclusion}
\markboth{General Conclusion}{}
\label{ch:conclusion}

This thesis addressed the automated grading of diabetic retinopathy in a context where
screening needs grow much faster than the number of human graders, and where an automated
system is only acceptable if it is both accurate and safety-oriented. Starting from a
review of a decade of deep-learning methods, we argued that three architectural
limitations recur across the representative systems reviewed in this thesis: they grade
each image in isolation, they do not use the global topology of the vascular network, and
they must answer even when the image is ambiguous. The work was organised around
addressing these three limitations together. Throughout, ``disease progression'' was
operationalised as the prediction of DR severity on the ordinal ICDR scale from a single
image; the work is cross-sectional, not longitudinal.

The central contribution of this thesis is the development and retrospective evaluation of
a three-axis architecture for diabetic retinopathy grading that jointly incorporates
relational information, retinal vascular topology, and ordinal prediction with selective
abstention. The study began with the clinical context of the disease and the
methodological tools needed for the three axes: graph neural networks, persistent
homology, ordinal regression and calibrated abstention. We then evaluated the graph
template outside medicine, on microservice and network-security graphs -- where a ten-run
re-evaluation showed that appended invariants bring interpretability rather than a
significant accuracy gain -- and gathered first evidence, with three independent methods,
that vascular and topological features of the retina carry information complementary to
deep visual features. These results led to
TOPORET, an inductive population graph whose edges combine visual and topological
similarity, and then to DOTS, which adds a retinal foundation model, an ordinal head with
guaranteed rank consistency and entropy-based abstention. Finally, the software of DOTS
was designed according to regulatory-oriented principles relevant to the IEC~62304 and
ISO~14971 standards and the EU AI Act.

The experiments provide three principal findings. First, the pre-specified analysis
supports the contribution of the population graph to grading performance (H1:
$t_4=3.82$, $p=0.009$, $+4.0$~pp QWK). Because the inferential unit is the cross-validation
fold, this test has four degrees of freedom; the bootstrap and exact permutation analyses
are complementary robustness checks on the same folds, not independent replications.
Second, persistent-homology descriptors extracted from retinal vascular structures show a
strong statistical association with ICDR severity (H2: $F(4,4995)=1308.9$,
$\eta^2=0.51$); this is an association, not evidence of a causal or biological mechanism.
Third, DOTS achieves a Sev-NR point estimate of $1.9\%$, below the predefined $2\%$
evaluation target, together with QWK $=0.951$, AUC $=0.988$, ECE $=0.040$, $93.8\%$
coverage and $0\%$ ordinal rank violations on the evaluated retrospective benchmarks
(H3a).

The interpretation of the third result requires particular caution. The 95\% confidence
interval for Sev-NR is $[1.4\%,\,2.4\%]$, whose upper bound remains above the predefined
target. Consequently, the present work provides evidence of favourable safety-oriented
performance toward the target, but does not establish statistical compliance with the
target or prospective clinical safety.

The H3b analysis further shows that, within the investigated architectural family and
experimental specification, the complete relational--structural--ordinal configuration is
the only evaluated configuration reaching the Sev-NR target at the point estimate while
satisfying the complete predefined criterion set. This constructive result should not be
interpreted as a proof that every possible architecture lacking one of the three axes
would fail.

The three research questions of the thesis can therefore be answered as follows. Does
the relational context between patients carry information beyond that of a per-image
classifier? In our experiments, yes, provided that the topology of the vessels
enters the graph, through both its similarity and its node features; a visual similarity
alone did not give a significant gain. Is the global topology of the vascular network related to the severity
of the disease? It is strongly associated with it, with the largest effects for the
loop-related features that plausibly reflect neovascularisation. Can a single architecture
meet all the predefined criteria of accuracy, calibration and safety, and does any reduced
version do so? The full architecture meets them at the point estimate, and none of the
reduced versions evaluated does, although the confidence interval of the safety criterion
calls for prospective confirmation.

Taken together, the findings support the scientific value of combining relational,
structural and ordinal-safety mechanisms in retrospective DR grading. They do not establish
clinical effectiveness, longitudinal disease-progression prediction, regulatory
certification, or readiness for routine clinical deployment. These questions require
prospective, multi-site clinical evaluation and subsequent regulatory assessment. The
thesis also introduces the use of persistent-homology descriptors of the vessel skeleton
inside the similarity of a population graph, and a validation discipline in which every
hypothesis, test and threshold is fixed before the experiment and no held-out test set is
used for any design choice. On the practical side, DOTS grades an image in $31.4$~ms under
the laboratory configuration of \cref{app:repro}, defers $6.2\%$ of images to human graders,
reports for each decision the most-attended similar cases and the topological features
(attention-derived explanatory signals, whose faithfulness remains to be evaluated), and
is designed to be verifiable and traceable. Under explicit prevalence assumptions, the
lower Sev-NR corresponds to a \emph{projected} reduction of about $600$ potentially
non-referred severe cases per year in an illustrative programme of $500{,}000$ patients;
this is a projection, not an observed outcome. A CPU model developed along the way
(SAMF-GB) also offers a low-cost alternative for settings without GPUs.

Beyond its results, the research programme taught several methodological lessons that may
be useful to other work on medical AI. First, accuracy and safety must be measured
separately: every system in this thesis gained QWK long before it reached the safety
target, and optimising the former does not guarantee the latter. Second, evaluating an
architectural idea outside medicine, where data are plentiful and labels unambiguous, made
the medical experiments easier to design and to interpret, although the transfer remains
an analogy; it also showed, through a negative result, that appending interpretable
descriptors does not by itself guarantee an accuracy gain, which is why the value of
topology on retinal data was tested separately. Third, fixing hypotheses, tests and thresholds before running the experiments
turned some results that could have been discarded, such as the non-significant
visual-only graph, into informative findings. Fourth, taking regulatory-oriented principles
into account from the start cost little and avoided late changes to the architecture.
Finally, the most useful component for safety was not the most accurate one:
entropy-based abstention adds less than one point of QWK, but it is the step that brings
the non-referral point estimate below the target.

\phantomsection\label{sec:limits-gc}
\paragraph{Limitations.}
These results must be read within clear limits.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{No prospective validation.} All experiments are retrospective, on curated
public datasets. The upper bound of the 95\% confidence interval of Sev-NR ($2.4\%$) is
above the $2\%$ target, so statistical compliance with the safety criterion is not
established, and no claim of prospective clinical safety or effectiveness is made.
\item \textbf{Image-level evidence, no demographic equity analysis.} Sample sizes are
counts of retinal images ($108{,}301$ across the five benchmarks), not necessarily of
independent patients, and none of the datasets provides demographic information, so
fairness across age, sex and ethnicity could not be assessed.
\item \textbf{No longitudinal validation.} The population graph uses a single visit per
patient; the thesis grades severity cross-sectionally and does not predict individual
disease trajectories.
\item \textbf{No regulatory certification.} \Cref{ch:reg} documents a
regulatory-oriented design and mapping, not a conformity assessment, clinical evaluation,
CE marking or FDA clearance. The integration with hospital information systems (FHIR
adapter) is designed but not implemented, and remains the main gap between the research
system and a clinical product.
\item \textbf{H3b is limited to the investigated family.} The necessity result holds for
the six proper subsets evaluated under one specification, not for every conceivable
architecture, and is contingent on the $2\%$ target.
\item \textbf{The transfer from distributed systems is an architectural analogy}, not a
formal transfer guarantee.
\end{enumerate}
In addition, performance is lower on the smallest dataset (IDRiD, $516$ images), where the
multi-seed Sev-NR of DOTS ($2.1\%$) is above the target; its small size and sparser
population graph are a plausible, but not demonstrated, explanation. The
attention weights used in the explanation template have not been evaluated for
faithfulness, and the computation of topological features is the main latency
bottleneck.

These limits open several directions for future work. The most immediate is a
prospective, multi-site reader study, pre-registered and powered on Sev-NR, to evaluate
DOTS in real screening conditions and to reduce the uncertainty on Sev-NR; it is the first
step of the planned four-phase translation roadmap of \cref{sec:roadmap}, which could lead
to a randomised trial and to a regulatory assessment. On the methodological side,
learnable topological layers could replace the hand-crafted persistence vector and
possibly improve accuracy, provided that they remain interpretable. A temporal population
graph, linking the successive visits of each patient, would allow genuine longitudinal
modelling of disease progression, for example at the difficult Grade~2/3 boundary.
Federated construction of the population graph would allow several hospitals to
collaborate without sharing patient data. Smartphone-based fundus capture, connected to
the DOTS inference service, could extend screening to low-resource settings. Finally, an
explanation layer based on large language models could turn the logged evidence into
narratives adapted to clinicians and patients, under strict safeguards that prevent it
from contradicting the grade or the decision to abstain.

More broadly, the three-axis framework -- relational graph, structural topology, and
ordinal prediction with abstention -- is not specific to the retina. It may serve as a
template for other graded diseases with an ordinal severity scale, a structural anatomical
prior and a safety target, such as age-related macular degeneration or glaucoma; this
remains to be tested.

In summary, the thesis does not claim that DOTS is clinically validated or
regulatory-ready; it demonstrates that, within the investigated architectural family,
relational, structural/topological and ordinal-safety mechanisms can be integrated into a
single system that achieves favourable safety-oriented performance on retrospective public
benchmarks. The code, models and configurations are released for independent
reproduction, and the remaining steps towards clinical use are clearly identified. The
prevention of blindness at the scale of a population will depend on the ability to carry
such systems from computational evaluation to prospectively validated clinical practice.
