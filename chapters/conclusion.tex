\lofgroup{General Conclusion}
\lotgroup{General Conclusion}

This thesis addressed the automated grading of diabetic retinopathy in a context where
screening needs grow much faster than the number of human graders, and where an automated
system is only acceptable if it is both accurate and safety-oriented. Starting from a
review of a decade of deep-learning methods, we argued that three architectural
limitations recur across the representative systems reviewed in this thesis. They grade
each image in isolation, they do not use the global topology of the vascular network, and
they are rarely trained or evaluated with the referral of severe cases in mind. The task
throughout was the prediction of DR severity on the ordinal ICDR scale from a single
image; the work is cross-sectional, not longitudinal.

The central contribution of this thesis is the development and retrospective evaluation of
relational, topological and ordinal-safety mechanisms for diabetic retinopathy grading.
The study began with the clinical context of the disease and the methodological tools
needed for the three axes: graph neural networks, persistent homology, ordinal regression
and calibration. We then evaluated the graph template outside medicine, on microservice
and network-security graphs, where a ten-run re-evaluation showed that appended invariants
bring interpretability rather than a significant accuracy gain. We also gathered first
evidence, with three independent methods, that vascular and topological features of the
retina carry information complementary to deep visual features. These results led to
TOPORET, a population graph whose edges combine visual and topological similarity. Two systems trained on a fused multi-source corpus then extended this work:
DGTS, a population graph regularised by persistent homology of feature-space
neighbourhoods, and DOTS, an ordinal pipeline evaluated against screening safety criteria.
Finally, the software of these pipelines was designed according to regulatory-oriented
principles relevant to the IEC~62304 and ISO~14971 standards and the EU AI Act. TOPORET,
DGTS and DOTS are thus three complementary contributions to one objective: exploiting the
relations between images, the topology of retinal structures and the order of the grades
to improve DR grading.

The experiments provide three principal findings. First, the pre-specified analysis
provides evidence supporting the contribution of the population graph to grading
performance (H1: $t_4=3.82$, $p=0.019$ two-tailed, $+0.04$ QWK, reproduced on three
benchmarks), and the
same direction of effect is observed on a second corpus with DGTS ($+0.018$ QWK for the
graph). Because the inferential unit of H1 is the cross-validation fold, its test has four
degrees of freedom; corrected for the overlap of the folds, it is no longer significant on
any single benchmark ($p=0.06$--$0.09$) but remains so for the three benchmarks combined
($p=0.015$); this is evidence, not proof, given the five folds. The TOPORET results were obtained transductively with image-level folds,
whereas the DGTS result is inductive. Second, persistent-homology descriptors extracted from
retinal vascular structures show a statistical association with ICDR severity (H2:
$F=84.3$, $p<0.001$, a small effect, $\eta^2\approx0.01$); this is an association, not
evidence of causality or of a demonstrated biological meaning. A topological signal also helps on the feature-space neighbourhoods
of DGTS ($+0.030$ QWK), although it cannot be interpreted anatomically there. Third, the observed performance of DOTS
satisfies the pre-set screening safety criteria at the point estimate, internally (QWK $=0.953$,
Sev-NR $=0.97\%$, PDR-NR $=1.27\%$) and on the independent Messidor-2 dataset (QWK
$=0.931$, Sev-NR $=1.4\%$, PDR-NR $=1.9\%$), whereas none of its four reduced variants
reaches Sev-NR $\leq2\%$ on Messidor-2 (H3a and H3b).

The interpretation of the third result requires particular caution. With $309$ severe
images, the exact 95\% interval of the internal Sev-NR is $[0.2\%,\,2.8\%]$, whose upper
bound remains above the $2\%$ threshold, and the external intervals cannot be computed from
the published counts. The present work therefore provides evidence of favourable
safety-oriented performance, but does not establish statistical compliance with the
threshold or prospective clinical safety. Moreover, DGTS shows that good generalisation in
QWK ($0.925$ on EyePACS) does not by itself guarantee the safety criterion: its Sev-NR on
EyePACS is about $2.1\%$.

The three research questions of the thesis can therefore be answered as follows. Does
the relational context between patients carry information beyond that of a per-image
classifier? In our experiments, yes, on two corpora: a graph built from visual similarity
alone already improves the QWK in DGTS, inductively, and in TOPORET the largest gain is
obtained when topology enters both the similarity and the node features. Is the global topology of the vascular network
related to the severity of the disease? It is statistically associated with it: total
$H_1$ persistence, which measures loops of the segmented vessel skeleton, increases
monotonically with severity; a link with neovascularisation is plausible but not
demonstrated. Can an ordinal pipeline meet screening safety criteria, and
does any reduced version do so? The observed performance of DOTS satisfies them at the point estimate internally and
externally, and none of the reduced versions evaluated does on external data, although the
confidence interval of the safety criterion calls for confirmation on larger data.

\paragraph{What the thesis shows, supports and does not evaluate.}
The experiments \emph{show directly}, under the stated protocols, the measured quantities:
a QWK gain of TOPORET in every fold of three benchmarks, the gains of the graph and of the
topological regulariser in the DGTS ablation, the external QWK of DGTS on EyePACS, point
estimates of DOTS that satisfy the screening targets internally and on Messidor-2, and the
failure of all four reduced variants on Messidor-2. They \emph{support, without
establishing conclusively}, three broader statements: that relational information improves
grading (five folds, corrected tests significant only in combination), that topology
carries severity-related information (a small statistical association), and that the
ordinal pipeline is safe enough for screening (an interval that crosses $2\%$). They
\emph{do not evaluate} clinical safety or effectiveness, patient-level TOPORET, multi-seed
variability, reader performance, fairness, longitudinal prediction or regulatory
compliance. The contribution that remains despite these limits is a structured body of
retrospective evidence on how relational, topological and ordinal information can
complement deep visual representations for DR grading. \Cref{tab:claimev} maps each claim
to its evidence and main limitation.

\paragraph{Scope of the claims.}
This thesis does not claim prospective clinical effectiveness, regulatory approval,
fairness across populations, longitudinal disease prediction or a general superiority of
graph-based models. It does not claim that the topological descriptors are anatomical
biomarkers of DR, nor that the three axes have been evaluated jointly in a single system.
Its conclusions are restricted to retrospective evaluation on the datasets and protocols
described here. \Cref{fig:evarch} shows the same boundary from the point of view of the systems: each
contributes a different kind of evidence to the common objective, and all of it stops
before prospective evaluation.

\input{figures/fig_evidence}

Beyond these findings, the thesis introduces the use
of persistent-homology descriptors of the vessel skeleton inside the similarity of a
population graph, and an evaluation discipline in which safety metrics are reported next to
accuracy and checked on data from another country. On the practical side, DGTS grades an
image in $18.3$~ms and DOTS in about $75$~ms on one T4 GPU, and both report quantities that
can be shown to a clinician -- similar cases with their grades, topological descriptors and
calibrated probabilities -- whose faithfulness as explanations remains to be evaluated.
In an illustrative operational projection under explicit prevalence assumptions, the
lower Sev-NR of DOTS corresponds to about $1{,}100$ fewer non-referred severe cases per
year in a programme of $500{,}000$ patients; this calculation is not an estimate of
clinical impact. A CPU model developed along the way (SAMF-GB) also offers a low-cost alternative for
settings without GPUs.

Beyond its results, the programme taught five methodological lessons. First, accuracy and
safety must be measured separately: DGTS generalises well in QWK yet misses the Sev-NR
threshold externally. Second, evaluating an architectural idea outside medicine made the
medical experiments easier to design, and its negative result showed that appended
descriptors do not by themselves guarantee an accuracy gain. Third, fixing hypotheses and
tests in advance turned modest or negative results into findings rather than results to
be explained away. Fourth, external data and released code are part of the evidence: only
the external evaluations are free of the uncertainty about the two training sources, and
only the code settles which protocol produced a result. Finally, the component that most
improves safety is not necessarily the one that most improves accuracy: the ordinal head of
DOTS adds the smallest QWK gain, yet only the complete pipeline reaches the Sev-NR threshold
externally.

\phantomsection\label{sec:limits-gc}
\paragraph{Limitations.}
These results must be read within clear limits.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Statistical uncertainty.} H1 rests on five folds; corrected for their
overlap, it is significant only for the three benchmarks combined. The internal Sev-NR
interval of DOTS extends to $2.8\%$, above the $2\%$ threshold, so the safety criteria are
satisfied at the point estimate only and safety is not demonstrated. The
cross-validation splits were drawn with one seed ($42$) and were not repeated with others.
\item \textbf{No prospective or clinical validation.} All experiments are retrospective, on
curated public datasets, and no claim of prospective clinical safety or effectiveness is
made.
\item \textbf{A transductive, image-level evaluation of TOPORET.} Following its released
code, TOPORET was evaluated with image-level folds and a graph that contains the test
images as unlabelled nodes, and its topological similarity compares six summary
statistics rather than full persistence diagrams. Part of its gain on Kaggle DR and
Messidor-2 may come from the fellow eye of the same patient; the inductive evidence for
the relational axis comes from DGTS, and the fold-overlap correction leaves H1 significant
only for the three benchmarks combined.
\item \textbf{A small multi-source corpus of uncertain independence.} DGTS and DOTS are
trained on $5{,}859$ images; KaggleDR-F1 has exactly $60\%$ of the APTOS~2019 count in
every grade, and the absence of shared images between the two sources has not been
verified, so the cross-validation results may be optimistic. The external validations
(EyePACS, Messidor-2) are not affected.
\item \textbf{Image-level evidence, no demographic equity analysis.} Sample sizes are
counts of retinal images, not necessarily of independent patients, and none of the datasets
provides demographic information, so fairness across age, sex and ethnicity could not be
assessed.
\item \textbf{No single three-axis model and no abstention.} The relational and topological
axes (TOPORET, DGTS) and the ordinal-safety axis (DOTS) are evaluated in separate
systems, and no system defers uncertain images to a human grader.
\item \textbf{No longitudinal validation.} The population graph uses a single visit per
patient; the thesis grades severity cross-sectionally and does not predict individual
disease trajectories.
\item \textbf{No regulatory certification.} \Cref{ch:reg} documents a
regulatory-oriented design and mapping, not a conformity assessment, clinical evaluation,
CE marking or FDA clearance. The integration with hospital information systems (FHIR
adapter) is designed but not implemented.
\item \textbf{The transfer from distributed systems is an architectural analogy}, not a
formal transfer guarantee.
\end{enumerate}
Three technical caveats complete this list. The non-referral definition counts a severe
image predicted as Moderate as referred, which is less strict than requiring a predicted
grade of at least~$3$. The topology used by DGTS is that of the feature space, not of the
vessels. Finally, the attention block of the DOTS feature refinement encoder is inactive
on sequences of length one, so no attention-based benefit is claimed.

\phantomsection\label{sec:persp-gc}
\paragraph{Perspectives.}
The experimental weaknesses listed above define the first perspectives, in order of cost.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Patient-level split.} Re-evaluate TOPORET with patient-level folds and a graph
built from the training images only, so that H1 is tested under the same inductive
conditions as DGTS; the released code needs only a grouped split and a per-fold graph.
\item \textbf{Multi-seed validation.} Repeat each experiment over several random seeds and
report the spread of QWK and Sev-NR, so that the gains are not tied to one initialisation.
\item \textbf{New external datasets.} Test DGTS and DOTS on further external datasets, with
enough severe images (about $650$ at the observed rate) for the upper bound of the Sev-NR
interval to fall below $2\%$.
\item \textbf{A unified model with deferral.} Combine the three ingredients in one model --
a population graph with vascular topology and a CORAL ordinal head -- and add a deferral
mechanism built on the calibrated probabilities of DOTS.
\item \textbf{Reader study.} Compare the system with human graders, and graders assisted
by the system, on the same images, including the usefulness of the similar cases and
topological descriptors shown to them.
\item \textbf{Prospective study.} Evaluate the model in a pre-registered prospective study
powered on the number of severe cases (about $1{,}240$, \cref{sec:roadmap}), the first
step of the translation roadmap.
\item \textbf{True multi-site and federated validation.} Replace the two-site simulation of
DOTS by a validation across hospitals, with a federated construction of the population
graph so that no patient data are shared.
\item \textbf{Clinical validation.} Only then can a clinical evaluation, in the sense of
the Medical Device Regulation, establish clinical safety and effectiveness.
\end{enumerate}
On the methodological side, retinal foundation models such as RETFound could replace the
ImageNet-pre-trained backbones; anatomically grounded persistent homology could replace
the feature-space descriptors of DGTS; and learnable topological layers could replace the
hand-crafted persistence vectors, provided that they remain interpretable. A temporal
population graph, linking the successive visits of each patient, would allow genuine
longitudinal modelling of disease progression, for example at the difficult Grade~2/3
boundary. Finally, an explanation layer based on large language models could turn the
logged evidence into narratives adapted to clinicians and patients, under strict
safeguards that prevent it from contradicting the grade.

More broadly, the three axes -- relational graph, structural topology, and ordinal
safety-oriented grading -- are not specific to the retina. They may serve as a template for
other graded diseases with an ordinal severity scale, a structural anatomical prior and a
safety target, such as age-related macular degeneration or glaucoma; this remains to be
tested.

In summary, the thesis does not claim clinically validated or regulatory-ready systems. It
shows, on retrospective public data with external validation, that relational, topological
and ordinal-safety mechanisms -- the three complementary contributions TOPORET, DGTS and
DOTS -- each bring a measurable benefit to DR grading, within the statistical limits stated
above, and it identifies the steps that remain: patient-level and multi-seed evaluation,
new external data, a unified model with deferral, and prospective, multi-site clinical
validation.
Preventing blindness at population scale will depend on carrying such systems from
computational evaluation to prospectively validated clinical practice.
