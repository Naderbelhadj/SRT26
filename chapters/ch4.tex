\section*{Introduction}
\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction}

TOPORET (\cref{ch:toporet}) provided evidence that a population graph and the topology
of the retinal vessel network improve DR grading. It was not, however, designed for safety: it
is trained on one dataset at a time, its softmax head ignores the order of the grades, and
its evaluation does not measure how many severe cases would be left unreferred. This
chapter addresses these three residual gaps with two systems developed on the same
multi-source corpus of $5{,}859$ fundus images:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{DGTS} (Deep Graph Topological System)~\cite{belhadj2026dgts} extends the
relational and topological axes of TOPORET: a $k$-nearest-neighbour population graph with
GraphSAGE aggregation, regularised during training by persistent-homology descriptors of
each node's neighbourhood, and validated without retraining on $35{,}126$ Kaggle EyePACS
images;
\item \textbf{DOTS} (Deep Ordinal Training System)~\cite{belhadj2026dots} addresses the
safety axis: ordinal supervision with CORAL, a residual Feature Refinement Encoder and
test-time augmentation, evaluated against screening safety thresholds internally and on
the independent Messidor-2 dataset.
\end{itemize}
The two systems share the data, the pre-processing and the cross-validation protocol, so
that their contributions can be compared, but they are distinct architectures. With
TOPORET, they pursue one objective: DGTS carries the relational and topological
ingredients to a multi-source corpus and to external data, and DOTS adds the ordinal one. The
chapter first reviews related work and states the residual gaps and hypothesis H3. It then
presents the shared protocol, DGTS, DOTS, a comparison of the two systems, a projection of
the clinical impact, and a discussion of what the results establish and what they do not.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:ch4-related}

\paragraph{Multi-source training.}
Most DR grading systems are trained and evaluated on a single public dataset, typically
APTOS~2019 or the 2015 Kaggle competition data~\cite{kaggle2015eyepacs,aptos2019}. A
single corpus of a few thousand images under-represents the rare severe grades and limits
generalisation to other cameras and populations. Pooling several sources increases the
number of minority-grade examples, but its benefit has rarely been isolated by a
controlled ablation.

\paragraph{Ordinal regression for DR grading.}
DR grading is often treated either as plain classification or as regression on the grade
value. Ordinal methods decompose the problem into cumulative binary decisions; among them,
CORAL~\cite{cao2020coral} enforces rank consistency through a shared weight vector and
ordered biases, while CORN~\cite{shi2023corn} models conditional probabilities and is rank
consistent when the cumulative probabilities are decoded as their chain-rule product.
Ordinal supervision is expected to concentrate errors on adjacent grades, which are the
least harmful clinically.

\paragraph{Safety metrics and external validation.}
The autonomous system cleared by the US Food and Drug Administration~\cite{abramoff2018pivotal}
was evaluated against pre-defined sensitivity and specificity endpoints, and national
programmes monitor the referral of sight-threatening disease~\cite{scanlon2017english}.
Research systems, in contrast, rarely report the non-referral rate of severe and
proliferative DR, and rarely evaluate on a dataset acquired with other cameras in another
country. Calibration of the predicted probabilities~\cite{guo2017calibration} is also
seldom reported, although it conditions the use of the confidence scores in triage.

\paragraph{Population graphs and topology as a regulariser.}
Population graphs connect similar patients so that their representations inform one
another~\cite{parisot2018disease,hamilton2017graphsage}. In TOPORET, persistent homology
describes the vessel skeleton and enters both the node features and the similarity
(\cref{ch:toporet}). A cheaper alternative, explored by DGTS, computes persistent homology
on the local neighbourhood of each node in the learned feature space and uses it as a
training-time regulariser, without vessel segmentation.

\section{Residual Gaps of TOPORET and Hypothesis H3}
\label{sec:ch4-motivation}\label{sec:ch4-gaps}

\Cref{fig:dots} summarises the two systems of this chapter and the gaps they address.

\input{figures/fig_dots}

\paragraph{Gap A -- single-source training.}
TOPORET is trained and evaluated on each benchmark separately (\cref{tab:crossdata}). Its
cross-dataset results are measured by retraining, not by applying a model trained on one
source to images from another. Both systems of this chapter are trained on a fused
APTOS~2019 $+$ KaggleDR-F1 corpus and evaluated on an external dataset that was never used
for training or model selection.

\paragraph{Gap B -- grade-agnostic loss.}
TOPORET uses a nominal softmax head trained with cross-entropy, which gives the same
penalty to a No-DR$\to$PDR error as to a No-DR$\to$Mild error. DOTS replaces it with the
CORAL ordinal head.

\paragraph{Gap C -- unmeasured safety.}
TOPORET reports accuracy, QWK and macro-F1, but not the rate at which severe or
proliferative cases would be left unreferred. Both systems of this chapter report these
non-referral rates, and DOTS is evaluated against screening safety thresholds on the
internal corpus and on Messidor-2.

\subsection{Hypothesis H3}
\label{sec:h3def}

The third hypothesis of the thesis concerns the safety axis and has two parts.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.4em,style=sameline,itemsep=2pt]
\item[H3a (sufficiency).] The complete DOTS pipeline satisfies the pre-set screening safety criteria
of \cref{tab:criteria} -- QWK $\geq0.80$, Sev-NR $\leq2\%$, PDR-NR $\leq2\%$ and no
Grade~$0\leftrightarrow4$ confusion -- both under five-fold cross-validation and on the
external Messidor-2 dataset.
\item[H3b (necessity within the ablation).] On Messidor-2, none of the reduced variants of
the DOTS ablation (CNN baseline, $+$GraphSAGE, $+$CORAL only, $+$TTA only) meets the
Sev-NR criterion.
\end{description}
The thresholds are those of the DOTS article~\cite{belhadj2026dots}. They are informed by
autonomous screening practice, whose pivotal trial used pre-defined sensitivity and
specificity endpoints~\cite{abramoff2018pivotal}, and by national programmes that monitor
the referral of sight-threatening disease~\cite{scanlon2017english}; they are evaluation
targets of this thesis, not published regulatory thresholds. H3 is evaluated at the point estimate; the confidence intervals are reported
and discussed in \cref{sec:ch4-discussion}.

\section{Shared Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:ch4-protocol}\label{sec:protocol}

\subsection{Multi-Source Corpus}

APTOS~2019~\cite{aptos2019} ($3{,}662$ images from Aravind Eye Hospital, India) and
KaggleDR-F1 ($2{,}197$ images with five-grade ICDR labels) are concatenated into a
corpus of $5{,}859$ images (\cref{tab:corpus}). Fusion increases the number of Severe images
from $193$ to $309$ and of PDR images from $295$ to $472$, a gain of about $60\%$ for both
safety-critical grades. APTOS~2019 provides one image per patient and KaggleDR-F1 contains
no bilateral pairs, so the image-level stratified split is also a patient-level
split for this corpus~\cite{belhadj2026dgts}.

\paragraph{A caveat on the two sources.}
KaggleDR-F1 contains exactly $60\%$ of the APTOS~2019 count in every grade
($1{,}083/1{,}805$, $222/370$, $599/999$, $116/193$, $177/295$), so the two collections have
identical grade distributions. This is compatible with KaggleDR-F1 being derived from
APTOS~2019, and the absence of shared images between the two sources has not been verified
in the underlying publications. If images were shared, some validation images would have
near-duplicates in the training folds, and the cross-validation estimates of this chapter
would be optimistic. The external evaluations on EyePACS (checked for duplicates) and on
Messidor-2 are not affected by this issue, which is why the conclusions of this chapter
rest primarily on them.

\subsection{Pre-processing, Splits and External Data}

All images are enhanced with CLAHE~\cite{zuiderveld1994clahe} (clip limit $2.0$, tiles
$8\times8$) applied to the grey-level channel and replicated to three channels. Both
systems use five-fold stratified cross-validation with random state $42$; the grade
proportions of each fold match those of the corpus. Class imbalance is handled by a
weighted random sampler with weights recomputed from each training fold, so that no
information from the validation fold is used. Two external datasets are used only for
evaluation:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Kaggle EyePACS}~\cite{kaggle2015eyepacs} ($35{,}126$ images) for DGTS,
evaluated zero-shot: no EyePACS image is used for training, graph construction or
hyperparameter selection, and a perceptual-hash comparison (Hamming distance $\leq4$)
found no duplicate between EyePACS and the training corpus~\cite{belhadj2026dgts};
\item \textbf{Messidor-2}~\cite{decenciere2014messidor} ($1{,}744$ images acquired in
France with Topcon cameras) for DOTS, never seen during training~\cite{belhadj2026dots}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Safety Metrics and Criteria}

A severe (Grade~3) or proliferative (Grade~4) image is counted as \emph{not referred} when
it is predicted as No DR or Mild, the two grades that do not trigger referral in a
screening pathway where moderate or worse DR is referable:
\begin{equation}
\text{Sev-NR} = \frac{\#\{i: y_i=3,\ \hat y_i\leq1\}}{\#\{i: y_i=3\}},\qquad
\text{PDR-NR} = \frac{\#\{i: y_i=4,\ \hat y_i\leq1\}}{\#\{i: y_i=4\}} .
\label{eq:nr}
\end{equation}
A severe image predicted as Moderate is therefore counted as referred. This definition is
less strict than one that would require a predicted grade of at least~$3$, and it must be
kept in mind when comparing Sev-NR values across studies. \Cref{tab:criteria} lists the
criteria of H3a.

\section{DGTS: Topology-Regularised Population Graph}
\label{sec:dgts}

\subsection{Architecture}

DGTS~\cite{belhadj2026dgts} has five stages. (1)~CLAHE pre-processing. (2)~A frozen
EfficientNet-B4~\cite{tan2019efficientnet} pre-trained on ImageNet encodes each image into
$f_i\in\mathbb{R}^{1792}$; the features are extracted once and cached. (3)~A directed
cosine $k$-nearest-neighbour graph with $k=8$ is built over the $\ell_2$-normalised
training features:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{N}(i) = \operatorname*{top-}k_{\,j\neq i}\ \frac{f_i^{\top}f_j}{\lVert f_i\rVert\,\lVert f_j\rVert}.
\label{eq:dgts-knn}
\end{equation}
(4)~A two-layer GraphSAGE head~\cite{hamilton2017graphsage} with mean aggregation maps
$\mathbb{R}^{1792}\to\mathbb{R}^{512}\to\mathbb{R}^{256}$,
\begin{equation}
h_{\mathcal{N}(i)}^{(l)}=\operatorname{MEAN}\{h_j^{(l-1)}: j\in\mathcal{N}(i)\},\qquad
h_i^{(l)}=\operatorname{ReLU}\!\bigl(\operatorname{BN}(W_l[h_i^{(l-1)}\,\Vert\,h_{\mathcal{N}(i)}^{(l)}])\bigr),
\label{eq:dgts-sage}
\end{equation}
and, in parallel, a persistent-homology branch described below is projected to
$q_i\in\mathbb{R}^{64}$. (5)~The concatenation $[h_i^{(2)};q_i]\in\mathbb{R}^{320}$ feeds a
five-class softmax head trained with focal loss ($\gamma=2$) and class-balanced sampling.

\paragraph{Persistent-homology regulariser.}
For each training node, the $k+1=9$ feature vectors of the node and its neighbours are
projected to two dimensions by truncated SVD, and the $H_1$ persistence diagram of their
Vietoris--Rips filtration is computed with GUDHI~\cite{maria2014gudhi}. The $16$ most
persistent pairs are encoded as $p_i=[b_1,d_1-b_1,\ldots,b_{16},d_{16}-b_{16}]\in\mathbb{R}^{32}$
(zero-padded) and projected by $q_i=\operatorname{ReLU}(W_p p_i)$. A tight, single-grade
neighbourhood produces no persistent loop, whereas a diffuse or mixed neighbourhood
produces loops with high persistence: the descriptor encodes the \emph{shape} of the
neighbourhood, which the mean aggregation of \cref{eq:dgts-sage} cannot recover. The
descriptor is computed for training nodes only; at inference $p_i=0$, so persistent
homology acts as a training-time regulariser rather than as an input feature. Because
$q_i=\mathrm{ReLU}(W_p p_i)$, the projected descriptor is zero at inference, and the softmax
head then relies on the graph representation $h_i^{(2)}$ alone; the topological branch acts
only through the gradients that shaped $h_i^{(2)}$ during training. All the figures reported
for DGTS, in cross-validation and on EyePACS, were obtained in this inference setting, so the
gain attributed to the regulariser is a gain of the deployed model, although its mechanism
is regularisation rather than the use of topology at inference.

\paragraph{Relation to TOPORET.}
The topology used here is that of the learned feature space, not of the retinal vessels:
its loops cannot be interpreted as vascular loops or neovascularisation. DGTS therefore
tests whether a topological signal helps a population graph when vessel segmentation is
not available; it does not replace the vascular analysis of \cref{ch:toporet}, and the
anatomical interpretation of H2 applies only to TOPORET. Unlike the
evaluation of TOPORET, which is transductive, DGTS is evaluated inductively: its graph is
built from the training fold only, and validation and external images are graded by
retrieving their neighbours among the training images, without entering the graph.

\paragraph{Training.}
AdamW~\cite{kingma2015adam} (learning rate $10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), cosine
annealing over $25$ epochs, batch size $16$, mixed precision, one NVIDIA T4 GPU. The graph is
rebuilt from each training fold and the descriptors are cached per fold; the checkpoint with
the best validation QWK of each fold is kept.

workers.

\section{DOTS: Ordinal Safety-Oriented Grading}
\label{sec:dots}

\subsection{Architecture}
\label{sec:ch4-arch}

DOTS~\cite{belhadj2026dots} has five stages. (1)~CLAHE pre-processing. (2)~An
EfficientNet-B3~\cite{tan2019efficientnet} backbone with ImageNet weights, fine-tuned end to
end on $320\times320$ images, produces $f_i\in\mathbb{R}^{1536}$. (3)~A linear projection
with GELU and dropout gives $z_i\in\mathbb{R}^{256}$. (4)~The Feature Refinement Encoder
(FRE) refines it:
\begin{equation}
h_i=\operatorname{LN}\bigl(z_i+\operatorname{FFN}(\operatorname{LN}(z_i+\operatorname{MHA}(z_i)))\bigr),
\label{eq:fre}
\end{equation}
where the feed-forward network maps $256\to512\to256$ with GELU and dropout~$0.2$.
(5)~A CORAL head maps $h_i$ to $K-1=4$ rank logits $\hat r_{ik}$, and the grade is
$\hat y_i=\sum_{k}\mathbb{1}[\sigma(\hat r_{ik})>0.5]$. At inference, predictions are
averaged over five test-time augmentations (identity, horizontal and vertical flips,
rotations by $90^{\circ}$ and $180^{\circ}$).

\paragraph{What the FRE is, and is not.}
The FRE follows the code of a post-norm transformer encoder block~\cite{vaswani2017attention},
but it operates on a sequence of length one. The attention weights are then identically
$1$, and the multi-head attention contributes no interaction between tokens: the FRE is
functionally a two-layer residual MLP with layer normalisation. Its benefit over a plain
linear projection, measured in the ablation below, comes from the non-linear bottleneck and
the normalised residual path, not from attention. The name of the system is therefore
expanded as \emph{Deep Ordinal Training System}, and no claim of attention-based benefit is
made.

\paragraph{CORAL loss.}
With $y_{ik}=\mathbb{1}[y_i\geq k]$, DOTS minimises
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CORAL}}=-\frac{1}{N(K-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}
\Bigl[y_{ik}\log\sigma(\hat r_{ik})+(1-y_{ik})\log\bigl(1-\sigma(\hat r_{ik})\bigr)\Bigr].
\label{eq:coral}
\end{equation}
The four binary tasks share all parameters except their biases, which structurally
encourages rank-consistent predictions (\cref{sec:coral}).

\paragraph{Training.}
AdamW (learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$), cosine annealing,
$12$ epochs per fold ($10$ in the controlled ablation), batch size $16$, mixed precision,
weighted random sampler recomputed per fold, one NVIDIA T4 GPU (about $45$~min per fold).
Inference with five augmentations takes about $75$~ms per image.

\subsection{Calibration and Significance}
\label{sec:dots-calib}

For the referral decision (Grade~$\geq2$ referable), the ECE is $0.041\pm0.006$, the Brier
score $0.072\pm0.004$ and the AUC $0.971$ $[0.963,\,0.979]$. For the five grades, the ECE

temperature scaling with $T=1.42$, fitted on each validation fold, reduces it to

CNN baseline gives $p=0.013$.

\subsection{What Is New in DOTS}
\label{sec:dots-new}
The components of DOTS are established ones: an ImageNet EfficientNet-B3, a residual
refinement block, a CORAL head and test-time augmentation. Its contribution is therefore
not a new layer but an empirical result obtained under one protocol: on external data,
only the combination of these components gives an observed performance that satisfies
the safety criteria, and the component
that improves safety most is not the one that improves QWK most (\cref{tab:ablate,tab:h3b}).
This kind of evidence is what accuracy-only benchmarks of individual components do not
provide.

\subsection{What the Results Do Not Establish}
\label{sec:notestablished}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{No statistical compliance with the safety thresholds.} The criteria are met
at the point estimate; with $309$ Severe images the internal interval extends to $2.8\%$,
and the external intervals cannot be computed from the published counts. At the observed
rate, about $650$ severe images would be needed for the upper bound to fall below $2\%$
(\cref{sec:roadmap}).
\item \textbf{A small corpus of uncertain independence.} $5{,}859$ training images, with
about $62$ Severe images per validation fold; possible overlap between KaggleDR-F1 and
APTOS~2019 has not been excluded, so the cross-validation results may be optimistic. Each
system was tested externally on only one dataset.
\item \textbf{A lenient non-referral definition.} A severe image predicted as Moderate is
counted as referred (\cref{eq:nr}); a stricter definition would give higher rates.
\item \textbf{No abstention.} Neither system implements a mechanism that defers uncertain
images to a human grader. The calibrated probabilities of DOTS make such a mechanism
possible, but it has not been evaluated.
\item \textbf{No single three-axis system.} The relational and topological axes (TOPORET,
DGTS) and the ordinal-safety axis (DOTS) are demonstrated in separate systems.
\item \textbf{Retrospective evidence only.} No prospective, multi-site or clinical
evaluation was conducted; the federated variant is a simulation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Outcome of the Hypotheses}

At the point estimate, the observed performance of DOTS satisfies the pre-set criteria --
QWK $\geq0.80$, Sev-NR $\leq2\%$, PDR-NR $\leq2\%$ and zero Grade~$0\leftrightarrow4$
confusion -- internally and on Messidor-2, which supports H3a; the interval of
\cref{sec:notestablished} shows that safety is not demonstrated. Within the ablation, no
reduced variant meets the Sev-NR criterion on Messidor-2, which supports H3b. DGTS
provides complementary evidence, on a second corpus, that relational and topological
information improve grading, while showing that good
generalisation in QWK does not by itself guarantee the safety criterion.

\section*{Conclusion}
\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Conclusion}

This chapter addressed the three residual gaps of TOPORET -- single-source training, a
grade-agnostic loss and unmeasured safety -- with two systems trained on the same
$5{,}859$-image multi-source corpus. DGTS, a population graph regularised by persistent
homology of feature-space neighbourhoods, reaches QWK $=0.941$ in cross-validation and
$0.925$ zero-shot on $35{,}126$ EyePACS images, with a Sev-NR of about $2.1\%$ on EyePACS.
DOTS, which combines EfficientNet-B3, a residual feature refinement encoder, CORAL ordinal
regression and test-time augmentation, reaches QWK $=0.9527$ with Sev-NR $=0.97\%$ and
PDR-NR $=1.27\%$ internally, and QWK $=0.931$ with Sev-NR $=1.4\%$ and PDR-NR $=1.9\%$ on
Messidor-2; none of its reduced variants meets the Sev-NR criterion externally. The
observed performance therefore satisfies the criteria of H3 at the point estimate and
within the evaluated ablation, without demonstrating safety; they are
retrospective and limited by the small number of severe cases. \Cref{ch:reg} describes the
software architecture of these pipelines and the regulatory-oriented principles that
guided it.
