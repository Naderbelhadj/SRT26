\section*{Introduction}
\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction}

Before building a complete grading system, two questions must be answered. First, does
a graph template that augments node features with interpretable node-level descriptors
behave sensibly on data where graphs and labels are unambiguous? Second, do global
structural and topological features of the retina actually carry information that a deep
network trained on the raw image does not already capture? If either answer were
negative, the systems of the following chapters would rest on weak foundations.

This chapter answers both questions with the first contributions of the thesis. In
Part~A, we evaluate the graph template on distributed-systems data, where graphs are
natural and ground truth is unambiguous: microservice orchestration, and anomaly
detection on a synthetic microservice benchmark and three intrusion-detection datasets.
We then explain which elements of this template are carried over to retinal grading, and
which are not. In Part~B, we move to diabetic retinopathy and test the value of structural
features with three independent studies: a hybrid CNN--GNN model with geometric and
topological descriptors, a gradient-boosting model on hand-crafted structural features
(SAMF-GB), and a compact hand-crafted pipeline evaluated on Messidor-2 (LightGBM-HC).
Weaker and negative intermediate results are reported deliberately, to show how the final
solution was built step by step.

\section{Part A: Evaluating the Graph Template Outside Medicine}
\label{sec:ch2-rationale}

Part~A evaluates the architectural template -- a $k$-nearest-neighbour graph over
entities, short-range message passing, and node features augmented with interpretable
node-level descriptors -- on non-medical data before any contact with retinal images.
The rationale is methodological: data where the graph is natural and the labels are
unambiguous make it possible to see what the template does and does not bring before
it is applied to patients. This evaluation is Contribution~C1.

\paragraph{Why distributed systems?}
Evaluating a graph architecture requires data where the graph is natural and the labels
are unambiguous. Microservice architectures and computer networks provide exactly this:
the dependencies between services or hosts form an explicit graph, and failures or attacks
are recorded without the inter-grader disagreement that affects medical labels. They also
share an essential property with a screening service: the system must make decisions on
new nodes as they appear. A template that behaves well in these conditions is a credible
candidate for a population graph of patients, and its weaknesses can be identified before
touching medical data.

\subsection{GNN-MSOrchest: Microservice Cascade-Failure Recovery}
\label{sec:msorchest}

GNN-MSOrchest~\cite{belhadj2025icaart} models a 48-service e-commerce microservice system
as a directed attributed graph $G=(V,E,X)$. Each node $v\in V$ carries a five-dimensional
health vector $x_v=[\mathrm{CPU},\mathrm{mem},\mathrm{lat},\mathrm{err},\mathrm{dep}]^\top$
updated every $200$~ms. Edges encode directed dependencies (service $u$ calls service
$v$) with propagation-delay attributes. A two-layer GraphSAGE with mean aggregation drives
adaptive workload allocation:
\begin{equation}
\hat y_v = \mathrm{MLP}\bigl(h^{(2)}_v\bigr),
\label{eq:msorch}
\end{equation}
where $h^{(2)}_v$ is the node representation after two message-passing rounds with
$d_h=256$ and $S=25$ sampled neighbours per layer. The prediction $\hat y_v$ of
\cref{eq:msorch} drives load-shedding decisions for node $v$ during cascade propagation.

\Cref{tab:msorch} compares GNN-MSOrchest with a static-threshold baseline. The cascade
isolation mechanism -- withholding load from nodes whose predictive entropy exceeds a
threshold -- is the methodological precursor of the deferral of uncertain images discussed
as future work in the General Conclusion.

The gain comes from using the state of neighbouring services: a threshold rule applied node
by node cannot anticipate an overload that is propagating through the dependency graph.
This relational effect is the one that the population graph exploits on retinal data.

\subsection{Local Invariants for Anomaly Detection}
\label{sec:invariants}

The second study~\cite{belhadj2026invariants}, extended in the book
chapter~\cite{belhadj2026lnaiinv}, augments the node features of a graph neural network
with \emph{local invariants}: node-level quantities that remain within stable bounds
during normal operation. A local invariant is a function of the attributes $A(v)$ of a
node and of those of its neighbours,
\begin{equation}
I(v) = f\bigl(A(v),\,\{A(u) : u\in\mathcal{N}(v)\}\bigr),
\quad I(v,t)\in\bigl[I_{\min}(v),\,I_{\max}(v)\bigr]\ \text{(normal)},
\label{eq:invariants}
\end{equation}
and three invariants are used: the request (or flow) success rate $I_1$, the local
centrality or normalised degree $I_2=|\mathcal{N}(v)|/|V|$, and a delay-based invariant
$I_3$ (average communication delay in the conference paper, inverse mean connection
duration in the extension). The augmented feature vector is
$x_v^{\mathrm{aug}}=[\,x_v\,\Vert\,I(v)\,]$, and the graph is a $k$-nearest-neighbour graph
($k=10$) on the standardised features.

\paragraph{Conference results.}
The conference paper~\cite{belhadj2026invariants} used a two-layer GCN (dropout $0.6$,
early stopping) on a synthetic benchmark of $1{,}000$ microservices ($100$ anomalous) and
on three intrusion-detection datasets: KDD Cup~1999~\cite{tavallaee2009nslkdd},
CICIDS~2017~\cite{sharafaldin2018cicids} and UNSW-NB15~\cite{moustafa2015unsw}.
\Cref{tab:invariants} reports its results: F1 between $94.4\%$ and $97.0\%$ with the
invariants and, in the comparison of detectors, an F1 of $97.0\%$ for the
invariant-augmented GCN against $93.0\%$ for the GCN alone and $94.4\%$ for a graph
attention network. These are single-run results.

\paragraph{Re-evaluation with ten runs: a null accuracy result.}
The extended study~\cite{belhadj2026lnaiinv} (AdaptInv-GNN) re-evaluated the approach with
a stricter protocol: two-hop simple graph convolution followed by an MLP, invariant bounds
updated online by exponentially weighted moving averages, and ten independent runs with
$95\%$ confidence intervals and Wilcoxon tests. Under this protocol the invariants do
\emph{not} improve F1: on UNSW-NB15, $94.79\%$ $[94.5,\,95.1]$ with invariants against
$94.91\%$ without ($\Delta=-0.12$~pp, $p=0.958$); on the synthetic benchmark, $90.77\%$
against $92.35\%$ ($\Delta=-1.58$~pp, $p=0.752$). No difference is statistically
significant. The contribution of the invariants is therefore \emph{interpretability}
(each alert is linked to a named quantity, such as the source time-to-live on UNSW-NB15)
and robustness to drift (under a controlled linear drift, adaptive bounds kept the false
alarm rate below $8\%$, against $41\%$ for a static threshold), not accuracy. The
extension explicitly revises the conference result, and this thesis adopts the revised
conclusion. The same study also notes that its evaluation is transductive: the test
nodes were present when the $k$-NN graph was built. The same invariants were also
incorporated into cybersecurity knowledge graphs combined with large language
models~\cite{belhadj2025ijcnn}, a direction outside the scope of this thesis.

\subsection{What Is Carried Over to Retinal Grading}
\label{sec:transfer}

Three elements of Part~A are carried over to retinal grading; the numerical
hyperparameters are not. First, the representation of entities as nodes of a
$k$-nearest-neighbour graph, with two rounds of neighbourhood aggregation. Second, the
augmentation of learned features with a small vector of interpretable descriptors: the
local invariants $I(v)$ become the topological feature vector $x_i^{\mathrm{TDA}}$ of
\cref{eq:tda}, so that $z_i=[\,x_i^{\mathrm{CNN}}\Vert x_i^{\mathrm{TDA}}\,]$. Third,
recall-prioritised detection of rare events, which becomes class-balanced training for the
rare severe grades. The graph model itself follows GNN-MSOrchest (GraphSAGE, $L=2$, $d_h=256$); TOPORET
aggregates all neighbours rather than a sample of $S=25$, and every hyperparameter used on
retinal data was re-examined on DR data (\cref{tab:hparam}).

\paragraph{From distributed systems to retinal grading.}
The correspondence between the objects of the two domains is set out in
\cref{tab:mapping} in pipeline order. The first three rows define the nodes and what they
carry: a service becomes a patient, the service health vector becomes the CNN embedding
of the fundus image, and the local invariants become the topological feature vector. The
fourth row defines the edges. The last three rows define behaviour: a cascade failure
becomes diagnostic uncertainty spreading across a grade boundary, load shedding would
become the deferral of uncertain images (not implemented in this thesis), and
recall-prioritised anomaly detection becomes class-balanced training that favours the rare
severe grades. The table is a design checklist, not evidence:
each correspondence is an analogy whose value on retinal data is tested in
\cref{ch:toporet,ch:dots}.

\paragraph{What the transfer claims and does not claim.}
The transfer is architectural and analogical, not distributional. It does not claim that
retinal population graphs and network graphs share statistical properties, nor that
performance on one guarantees performance on the other, nor that hyperparameters can be
reused without re-tuning. Part~A also carries a lesson that shapes the rest of the thesis:
appending interpretable descriptors to rich learned features may add interpretability
without adding accuracy, as the null result of the extended study shows. Whether the
topological descriptors add accuracy on retinal data is therefore tested separately, by
ablation (\cref{tab:ablation3}) and by the pre-specified test of H1.

\subsection{Threats to the Transfer Argument}
\label{sec:threats}

Three threats deserve to be named. First, the benchmarks of Part~A are graphs over
discrete events or entities (network flows, service calls); a population graph over
patients carries node features of a different statistical character. Second, the
analogue of the assumption that neighbours are informative -- that similar patients share
diagnostic information -- is precisely what H1 tests, not a premise this chapter can
establish. Third, the evidence of Part~A is limited: four anomaly-detection datasets, one
simulated orchestration testbed, a transductive evaluation, and an accuracy benefit of
the invariants that did not survive the ten-run re-evaluation. Part~A therefore motivates
the design; it does not validate it.

\section{Part B: Structural Evidence on Retinal Data}
\label{sec:partb}

Part~B presents three proof-of-concept studies of the structural axis (the answer to L2):
a deep hybrid model with geometric and topological descriptors, gradient boosting with
SHAP attribution on hand-crafted structural features, and a compact hand-crafted pipeline
with a family-level ablation. Each indicates that global vascular structure carries
information useful for DR grading. This convergence motivates the formal statistical test
of H2 in \cref{ch:toporet}.

\paragraph{Experimental setting.}
The three studies of Part~B use public datasets of \cref{sec:datasets}, but not the same
ones. The hybrid CNN--GNN model was developed on APTOS~2019 (distributed on Kaggle as
``Diabetic Retinopathy 224$\times$224 (2019 data)''), with binary screening (No DR versus
any DR) as the main task. SAMF-GB was evaluated on the five-grade task on Kaggle EyePACS,
a benchmark used for TOPORET in \cref{ch:toporet} and for the external validation of
DGTS in \cref{ch:dots}. LightGBM-HC was
evaluated on the five-grade task on Messidor-2. The three studies therefore differ in
data, task and input representation, and their results are compared qualitatively rather
than numerically.

\subsection{Hybrid CNN--GNN with Geometric and Topological Invariants}
\label{sec:hybrid}

The first retinal study~\cite{belhadj2026hybrid}, extended in the book
chapter~\cite{mezghich2026lnaitopo}, tests whether global structural descriptors add
information to a deep CNN--GNN pipeline. An EfficientNet-B0 backbone extracts local visual
features, a graph neural network models relations between image regions, and two families
of global descriptors are added: curvature-based geometric descriptors of the vessels,
computed from Gaussian-smoothed image derivatives, and persistent-homology invariants (the
lifespans of $H_0$ and $H_1$ features of a Rips complex built on salient vascular points).
In the conference version~\cite{belhadj2026hybrid}, the graph module is a two-layer graph
attention network over a $k$-nearest-neighbour graph ($k=8$) of $300$ superpixel
centroids, complemented by a Transformer encoder on image patches; the streams are fused
and classified by a fully connected layer.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
The main evaluation uses APTOS~2019 ($3{,}662$ images, resized to $128\times128$ pixels to
keep the persistent-homology computation tractable), with a stratified $70/15/15$ split,
on the binary screening task. Five-class grading is a secondary analysis, and Messidor-2
($1{,}748$ images) is used in the extended version, without fine-tuning, to test
generalisation under domain shift.

\paragraph{Ablation.}
\Cref{tab:hybrid} reports the ablation of the extended version~\cite{mezghich2026lnaitopo},
which adds each component in turn to the CNN baseline. Relational modelling by the GNN
gives the largest single gain ($+3.4$~pp); geometric and topological descriptors add
$+1.2$ and $+1.7$~pp respectively. The full model reaches $95.1\%$ accuracy (AUC $0.970$,
95\% bootstrap interval $[94.0\%,\,96.2\%]$), $+7.0$~pp over the baseline, which is more
than the sum of the individual gains ($6.3$~pp), and McNemar's test indicates a
significant improvement over the CNN baseline ($p<0.01$).

On Messidor-2, without any fine-tuning, the full model reaches $92.1\%$ accuracy against
$88.1\%$ for the CNN alone, and it keeps a higher AUC under Gaussian noise, contrast
changes, blur and downsampling~\cite{mezghich2026lnaitopo}. The conference
paper~\cite{belhadj2026hybrid} reports the same end points ($88.1\%$ for the CNN, $95.1\%$
for the full model) with a different decomposition: CNN $+$ GNN $91.5\%$, CNN $+$
Transformer $92.1\%$, and CNN $+$ GNN $+$ Transformer $95.1\%$.

\paragraph{Five-class grading and training behaviour.}
On the harder five-class task, the full model reaches $87.5\%$ accuracy, with macro
precision, recall and F1 all at $87.3\%$~\cite{belhadj2026hybrid}. \Cref{fig:hybridcm}
shows where the errors lie. No~DR is almost always recognised ($1{,}795$ correct images).
The largest error flows pull the two advanced grades towards Moderate: $61$ Severe and $63$
Proliferative images are predicted as Moderate. They are followed by confusions between
Mild and Moderate ($71$ and $49$ images), whereas confusions between Severe and
Proliferative are fewer ($27$ and $32$).

Read against the safety criteria of \cref{ch:dots}, the errors towards Moderate would still
lead to referral, but $13$ of the $295$ Proliferative images are predicted as Mild or No~DR
($4.4\%$), above the $2\%$ threshold adopted later; this study did not target safety. At a
resolution of $128\times128$ pixels, the fine signs of the advanced grades, such as new
vessels and intraretinal microvascular abnormalities, are hard to resolve, which is
consistent with the pull towards Moderate.

\paragraph{Topological descriptors of the vessel skeleton.}
The later systems of this thesis describe the vascular network with a small fixed vector
of persistence statistics: a six-dimensional vector in TOPORET (\cref{eq:tda-toporet})
and the seven-dimensional vector below in SAMF-GB (\cref{sec:samfgb}). For each fundus image, the vessel skeleton is extracted with CLAHE
pre-processing~\cite{zuiderveld1994clahe} followed by Zhang--Suen
thinning~\cite{zhang1984thinning}. The branch points of the skeleton form a point cloud
$P_i=\{p_1,\ldots,p_n\}\subset\mathbb{R}^2$. The Vietoris--Rips filtration is computed
with GUDHI~\cite{maria2014gudhi}, which gives a seven-dimensional feature vector
\begin{equation}
x_i^{\mathrm{TDA}} = \bigl(\Pi_{H_0},\, n_{H_0},\, \Pi_{H_1},\, n_{H_1},\, \beta_1,\,
D_f,\, \Delta\bigr)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^7 ,
\label{eq:tda}
\end{equation}
where $\Pi_{H_k}$ is the total $k$-th persistence, $n_{H_k}$ the number of components
or loops, $\beta_1$ the first Betti number, $D_f$ the fractal dimension, and $\Delta$ the
maximum $H_1$ persistence (a neovascularisation signal).

The synergy observed in \cref{tab:hybrid} motivates the next step. In the hybrid model,
the graph links regions within one image and the topological descriptors are simply
concatenated; TOPORET (\cref{sec:dualsim}) instead builds a graph between patients and uses
a topological distance inside the similarity that defines its edges, fusing both signals at
graph-construction time.

\subsection{SAMF-GB: Structural Features Outperform GPU EfficientNet-B3}
\label{sec:samfgb}

SAMF-GB~\cite{belhadj2026samfgb} shows that seven families of hand-crafted structural
features, combined with LightGBM gradient boosting~\cite{ke2017lightgbm}, outperform a
GPU EfficientNet-B3, with a classifier about $13$ times cheaper to run (feature extraction
excluded, \cref{tab:samf}). The 256-dimensional
feature vector is composed as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{vascular morphology} (48-D): vessel calibre reduction and tortuosity;
\item \textbf{fractal dimension} (12-D): loss of vascular self-similarity;
\item \textbf{persistent homology} (7-D): topology of the vessel network (\cref{eq:tda});
\item \textbf{DWT texture} (64-D, four levels): microaneurysm and exudate texture;
\item \textbf{Gabor responses} (32-D): vessel edges and IRMA characteristics;
\item \textbf{radial FFT} (24-D): degradation of the spoke-wheel vessel pattern;
\item \textbf{LBP $+$ log-Euclidean} (69-D): haemorrhage and exudate cluster patterns.
\end{itemize}

\Cref{tab:shap} gives the dimension and mean absolute SHAP value~\cite{lundberg2017shap}
of each family. The three most discriminative families are all global structural
features (vascular morphology, fractal dimension, persistent homology), not local
appearance features. This is supporting evidence for H2, obtained with a method entirely
different from the ANOVA of \cref{sec:h2}.

\subsection{LightGBM-HC: A Compact Hand-Crafted Pipeline on Messidor-2}
\label{sec:lgbmhc}

Publication~\cite{belhadj2026icpram} evaluates a compact hand-crafted pipeline on
Messidor-2 ($1{,}748$ images). After circular cropping, resizing, intensity
normalisation and CLAHE on the green channel, a $210$-dimensional descriptor is built from
seven families: multiscale Frangi vesselness statistics and skeleton-based vascular
geometry, box-counting fractal dimension (global and per quadrant), Gabor responses, local
binary patterns, radial FFT statistics, multilevel discrete wavelet energies, and
log-Euclidean covariance descriptors. The descriptor contains no persistent-homology
feature. A LightGBM classifier with a multiclass objective is trained with stratified
five-fold cross-validation and early stopping; ordinal consistency is assessed through the
QWK. The pipeline reaches QWK $=0.858$, accuracy $89.7\%$ and macro F1 $0.895$, against a
QWK of $0.447$ for a simple stacking baseline (logistic regression and random forest), with
a classifier inference time of about $1.6$~ms per image on CPU; feature extraction
dominates the run time. \Cref{tab:lgbm-hp} lists the main hyperparameters.

A family-level ablation removes the major feature groups one at a time

removed), followed by the vesselness (vascular) features ($-0.057$), the frequency-domain
features ($-0.029$) and the fractal descriptors ($-0.024$). Vascular structure is therefore
informative on Messidor-2, but in this pipeline it does not dominate multiscale texture;
the study supports the value of vascular descriptors, not specifically of topology.

\subsection{Convergent Evidence from Four Methodologies}
\label{sec:converge}

Four approaches -- the three studies of Part~B and the formal ANOVA of
\cref{ch:toporet} -- point in the same qualitative direction: global vascular structure
carries information useful for DR grading.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Deep learning}~\cite{belhadj2026hybrid,mezghich2026lnaitopo}: topological
descriptors add $+1.7$~pp beyond the CNN baseline (\cref{tab:hybrid}).
\item \textbf{Gradient boosting}~\cite{belhadj2026samfgb}: SAMF-GB outperforms GPU
EfficientNet-B3 by $+0.042$ QWK; persistent homology ranks third of seven families by SHAP
(\cref{tab:shap}).
\item \textbf{Compact hand-crafted pipeline}~\cite{belhadj2026icpram}: on Messidor-2,
removing the vesselness or fractal families lowers the QWK ($-0.057$ and $-0.024$,
\cref{tab:lgbm-abl}); this study contains no topological feature.
\item \textbf{Formal ANOVA} (\cref{sec:h2}): total $H_1$ persistence differs across grades,
$F=84.3$, $p<0.001$.
\end{enumerate}
Only items~1, 2 and~4 concern topology specifically; item~3 concerns vascular structure
more broadly.

\paragraph{Why four methodologies, not one.}
Each methodology has its own failure mode: a deep-learning ablation (\cref{tab:hybrid})
can be confounded by optimisation and a single split, a SHAP attribution
(\cref{tab:shap}) by correlated features, a family-level ablation (\cref{tab:lgbm-abl}) by
interactions between families. Agreement across such methods is harder to explain away
than one large effect; as the datasets differ, it is qualitative.

\section{Synthesis of the First Contributions}
\label{sec:ch3-synthesis}

\Cref{tab:ch3summary} summarises the five studies of this chapter.

Three design decisions for the rest of the thesis follow from these studies. The graph
template (entities as nodes of a $k$-NN graph, short-range aggregation, interpretable
descriptors appended to learned features, isolation of uncertain nodes) is carried over
as a design, while its hyperparameters are re-examined on retinal data; Part~A also
warns that appended descriptors may bring interpretability without accuracy, so their
accuracy benefit must be tested on retinal data. Topological and vascular descriptors
deserve a place in the model, since several methods found them informative. Finally, the
way the two signals are combined matters: since they already interact favourably when
simply concatenated, the next system goes further and fuses topology into the similarity
that builds a graph between patients.

\section*{Conclusion}
\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Conclusion}

This chapter established three findings. (F1)~Outside medicine, the graph template behaves
well on anomaly-detection data (F1 between $94.4\%$ and $97.0\%$ in the conference study),
but the ten-run re-evaluation shows that appending local invariants brings
interpretability and robustness to drift rather than a significant accuracy gain; the
transfer to retinal grading is therefore a design analogy, not a validated result.
(F2)~On DR data, relational and topological contributions appear complementary: the full
hybrid model gains $+7.0$~pp over the CNN baseline, more than the sum of the individual
gains ($6.3$~pp) on a single split. (F3)~Global vascular structure carries discriminative
information, as indicated by a deep hybrid model, by gradient boosting with SHAP
attribution and by a family-level ablation on another dataset. As a practical by-product,
SAMF-GB reaches a higher QWK than a GPU EfficientNet-B3 with a gradient-boosting classifier
that runs on a standard CPU ($2.1$~ms per image once the features are extracted).

These results do not prove that a population graph will work on retinal images, but they
show that it is worth testing, with topology fused into the similarity that builds the
graph. The next chapter implements this idea in TOPORET.
