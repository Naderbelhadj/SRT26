\section*{Introduction}
\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction}

The previous chapter showed that global structural features carry useful information for
DR grading, and that graph and topological signals are worth combining. This chapter turns
these observations into a complete grading framework, TOPORET~\cite{belhadj2025plos}.
TOPORET connects patients in a population graph, processed by the inductive GraphSAGE
architecture, whose edges combine two similarities: how alike the fundus images look to a CNN, and how alike their vessel
networks are topologically. The same topological descriptors are also appended to the
features of each patient, so that topology plays a double role. This thesis introduces
TOPORET as a DR grading system that addresses the relational limitation (L1) and the
structural limitation (L2) at the same time; we are not aware of an earlier system
combining these two elements in a single population graph. The chapter reports
the framework and the results of the corresponding publication~\cite{belhadj2025plos}.
A shorter conference version~\cite{belhadj2026enase}, which focuses on the software
framework, used an earlier experimental configuration (visual--topological weight $\alpha=0.7$) and
therefore reports slightly different figures (for example $F=47.3$ for the
$H_1$-persistence ANOVA). All values in this chapter come from the protocol
of~\cite{belhadj2025plos} and of its released code (stratified five-fold cross-validation,
seed $42$, $\alpha=0.6$, $\tau=0.65$), and the two sets of figures should not be mixed.

We first review related work on population graphs and topological features in medical
imaging. We then state the two hypotheses tested in this chapter, present the pipeline and
the dual-similarity graph, and report the experiments: the tests of the hypotheses, the
results on three benchmarks, the ablation, the analysis of the graph and the comparison
with the literature. We close with a discussion of what the results do and do not imply.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:ch3-related}

\paragraph{Population graphs in medical imaging.}
Representing a cohort as a graph whose nodes are patients was popularised for disease
prediction from brain imaging, where graph convolutional networks combine imaging features
with phenotypic similarity between subjects~\cite{parisot2018disease}. The idea is that
similar patients tend to share a diagnosis, so the graph can propagate information from
labelled to unlabelled subjects. These approaches are usually transductive: the whole
cohort, including the test patients, must be present when the model is trained, which is
not compatible with a screening service where new patients arrive every day. In the DR
grading work reviewed here, graph-based approaches either model regions within one image
or connect patients through visual similarity only; TOPORET adds a topological term to
this similarity.

\paragraph{Topological features in medical imaging.}
Persistent homology has been used to describe the shape of anatomical structures and to
feed learning models with stable, interpretable descriptors~\cite{hofer2017topological,
carriere2020perslay}. For the retina, the complexity of the vascular tree, often summarised
by its fractal dimension~\cite{masters2004fractal}, is known to change with disease. To our
knowledge, persistence descriptors of the vessel skeleton had not been used as edge
information in a population graph before this work.

\section{Problem Formulation and Hypotheses}
\label{sec:ch3-problem}\label{sec:hyp}

Let $\{(I_j,y_j)\}$ be a set of fundus images with ICDR grades $y_j\in\{0,\ldots,4\}$. The
task is to predict the grade $\hat y_i$ of a new image $I_i$ that was not seen during
training, using both its own content and the content of similar patients. The chapter tests
two pre-specified hypotheses; their tests and decision thresholds were fixed before the
experiments of this chapter were run.

\paragraph{H1 -- The Relational Hypothesis.}
A population graph connecting patients through a dual-similarity metric (visual
embedding similarity $+$ topological distance) provides a statistically significant
improvement over independent per-image classification.
\emph{Pre-specified test:} paired two-tailed $t$-test across the five cross-validation folds, TOPORET
versus the CNN baseline, on the QWK; decision threshold $|t_4| > 2.78$
($t_{0.025,4}$, that is $\alpha=0.05$ two-tailed).

\paragraph{H2 -- The Structural Hypothesis.}
$H_0$ and $H_1$ persistent-homology features of the retinal vascular skeleton are
associated with ICDR severity grade.
\emph{Pre-specified test:} one-way ANOVA of the primary feature, total $H_1$ persistence
$\Pi_{H_1}$, across the five grades on Kaggle DR, with Tukey HSD post-hoc comparisons;
conservative significance level $\alpha=0.005$, critical value $F_{0.005;\,4,\,\infty}
\approx 3.72$.

The two hypotheses are complementary: H1 asks whether relations between patients improve
the grade, and H2 whether the vascular topology that enters these relations is itself
related to severity. Their formal statements are collected in \cref{app:stats}.

\section{Proposed Approach}
\label{sec:ch3-approach}

\subsection{Overview}
\label{sec:ch3-overview}

TOPORET~\cite{belhadj2025plos} addresses L1 with a population graph and L2 with
persistent homology, in a single pipeline. \Cref{fig:toporet} shows its six stages, the
typed interface between consecutive stages, and its headline results.

Three design principles guide TOPORET. First, the graph is \emph{inductive}: a new patient
is connected to the existing cohort at inference time, without retraining, which is what a
screening service requires. Second, topology is used twice: as a feature of each patient
and inside the similarity that decides which patients are neighbours. Third, each stage
has a typed input and output, so that it can be tested, replaced or audited on its own;
this modularity is described as a software framework in~\cite{belhadj2026enase} and
reused in \cref{ch:dots,ch:reg}.

\paragraph{Stage S1: pre-processing.}
Each image is converted to grey levels, enhanced with CLAHE~\cite{zuiderveld1994clahe} (clip
limit $2.0$, $8\times8$ tiles), a morphological top-hat transform (disc of radius~$7$) and a
closing (radius~$1$), normalised to zero mean and unit variance, and resized to
$300\times300$ pixels. A binary vessel mask is obtained by Otsu thresholding, and Zhang--Suen
thinning~\cite{zhang1984thinning} gives a one-pixel skeleton; components smaller than
$50$ pixels are removed as artefacts. \Cref{fig:clahe} shows the effect of CLAHE, and
\cref{fig:skeleton} follows stages S1--S3 on one image.

\paragraph{Stages S2--S4: visual and topological features.}
EfficientNet-B3~\cite{tan2019efficientnet}, pre-trained on ImageNet and kept frozen, gives a
visual embedding $x_i^{\mathrm{CNN}}\in\mathbb{R}^{1536}$; a backbone comparison on the
validation data of Kaggle DR (VGG-16, ResNet-50, EfficientNet-B0 and~B3) favoured
EfficientNet-B3~\cite{belhadj2025plos}. The branch points of the skeleton form a point cloud
$P_i$ (\cref{fig:skeleton}b), on which a Vietoris--Rips filtration is computed with
GUDHI~\cite{maria2014gudhi}, giving the persistence diagram of \cref{fig:skeleton}c.
From each persistence diagram $D_i^{H_0}$ and $D_i^{H_1}$, three statistics are computed:
the total persistence $\Pi=\sum_f (d_f-b_f)$, the persistence entropy
$E=-\sum_f p_f\log p_f$ with $p_f=(d_f-b_f)/\Pi$, and the number $n_\ell$ of long-lived
features, whose lifetime exceeds $5\%$ of the diameter of $P_i$. This gives the
six-dimensional vector
\begin{equation}
x_i^{\mathrm{TDA}} = \bigl(\Pi_{H_0},\, E_{H_0},\, n_\ell^{H_0},\, \Pi_{H_1},\, E_{H_1},\,
n_\ell^{H_1}\bigr)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^6 ,
\label{eq:tda-toporet}
\end{equation}
and the node feature vector
$z_i=[\,x_i^{\mathrm{CNN}}\,\Vert\,\mathrm{Norm}(x_i^{\mathrm{TDA}})\,]\in\mathbb{R}^{1542}$,
in which the two parts are concatenated as computed.
SAMF-GB uses a different, seven-dimensional topological vector (\cref{eq:tda}), and DGTS
computes persistent homology on feature-space neighbourhoods rather than on the vessels
(\cref{ch:dots}).

\subsection{Dual-Similarity Population Graph}
\label{sec:dualsim}

The similarity between two patients combines the cosine similarity of visual embeddings
with a topological term:
\begin{equation}
S_{ij} = \alpha\cdot\cos\bigl(x_i^{\mathrm{CNN}},\, x_j^{\mathrm{CNN}}\bigr)
       + (1-\alpha)\cdot\exp\bigl(-d_{ij}/d_{\max}\bigr),
\label{eq:dualsim}
\end{equation}
where $d_{ij}$ is the Euclidean distance between the $\ell_2$-normalised topological vectors
$x_i^{\mathrm{TDA}}$ and $x_j^{\mathrm{TDA}}$ of \cref{eq:tda-toporet}, and $d_{\max}$ its maximum over
the cohort. In the publication~\cite{belhadj2025plos}, this term is presented as a
surrogate of the $2$-Wasserstein distance $W_2(D_i,D_j)$ between persistence diagrams; the
released implementation computes it on the six summary statistics, which is cheaper but
retains only the information of these statistics, and this is the version evaluated here.
An edge
$(v_i,v_j)$ is added when $S_{ij}>\tau$. The two parameters were selected on the validation
data by a grid search (\cref{tab:hparam}): $\alpha=0.6$ and $\tau=0.65$. Across the grid,
validation accuracy ranges from $93.9\%$ to $95.5\%$, and every setting adjacent to the
selected one stays within $1.1$ points of it, so the results are not a knife-edge
consequence of one setting. A lower $\alpha$ gives more weight to topology in the
similarity, and a higher $\tau$ keeps only the most similar pairs. The selected setting
gives an average degree of about $12$ (\cref{tab:graphstruct}): large enough for information
to propagate between patients, and small enough to keep the graph sparse; a lower
threshold admits more edges between less similar patients, which the sensitivity grid
penalises.

\subsection{Stage S6: Graph Learning}
\label{sec:ch3-gnn}

A two-layer GraphSAGE~\cite{hamilton2017graphsage} with mean aggregation over all
neighbours, hidden dimension $256$, batch normalisation, ReLU activations and dropout $0.3$
refines the node representations; a linear layer and a softmax give the five grade
probabilities. Because about three quarters of the Kaggle DR images are Grade~0 ($73.5\%$,
\cref{tab:datasets}), the model is trained with a class-weighted cross-entropy,
$w_c=N/(C\,N_c)$ for grade $c$. Training is full-batch on the whole graph, with
Adam~\cite{kingma2015adam} (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, cosine annealing
to $10^{-5}$), at most $200$ epochs and early stopping on the validation loss (patience~$20$). The loss is nominal, not
ordinal: TOPORET does not model the order of the grades, a limitation addressed by DOTS
in \cref{ch:dots}.

Each epoch computes the representations of all the nodes in one pass, and the loss is
evaluated on the training nodes of the fold only; the validation nodes select the epoch by
early stopping, and the test nodes never enter the loss. For each fold, the model with the
lowest validation loss is kept and its predictions on the test nodes are scored. The
baseline and the ablation variants use the same loop: a variant without graph replaces
GraphSAGE by the two-layer perceptron, and a variant without topology removes the six
topological features from the node vectors, while the graph, when present, keeps its dual
similarity (\cref{tab:ablation3}).

\Cref{alg:toporet} summarises how TOPORET builds the population graph and grades a new
patient. Training images are processed once; a new image only requires its own features,
its connection to the existing graph and one GraphSAGE forward pass.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{TOPORET: population-graph construction and inductive grading.}
\label{alg:toporet}
\DontPrintSemicolon
\KwIn{training images $\{I_j\}$ with grades $\{y_j\}$; new image $I_i$; parameters $\alpha=0.6$, $\tau=0.65$}
\KwOut{predicted grade $\hat y_i$}
\BlankLine
\ForEach{training image $I_j$}{
  $\tilde I_j \leftarrow \mathrm{Enhance}(I_j)$; $P_j \leftarrow \mathrm{Skeleton}(\tilde I_j)$\tcp*{stage S1}
  $x_j^{\mathrm{CNN}} \leftarrow \mathrm{EfficientNetB3}(\tilde I_j)$\tcp*{stage S2}
  $D_j \leftarrow \mathrm{RipsPersistence}(P_j)$; $x_j^{\mathrm{TDA}} \leftarrow \phi(D_j)$\tcp*{stage S3, \cref{eq:tda-toporet}}
  $z_j \leftarrow [\,x_j^{\mathrm{CNN}} \Vert x_j^{\mathrm{TDA}}\,]$\tcp*{stage S4}
}
Build $G$: add the edge $(v_j,v_l)$ whenever $S_{jl}>\tau$ (\cref{eq:dualsim})\tcp*{stage S5}
Train the two-layer GraphSAGE with the class-weighted cross-entropy\tcp*{stage S6}
\BlankLine
Compute $z_i$ for the new image $I_i$ with stages S1--S4\;
Connect $v_i$ to the training nodes $v_j$ with $S_{ij}>\tau$\;
$h_i \leftarrow \mathrm{GraphSAGE}(z_i,\{z_j\}_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)})$\tcp*{\cref{eq:sage-agg,eq:sage-upd}}
\Return{$\hat y_i \leftarrow \arg\max_c \mathrm{softmax}(h_i)_c$}
\end{algorithm}

The first part of \cref{alg:toporet} (lines~1--7) is run once, at training time; the second
part (lines~8--11) is all that is needed to grade a new patient, which is why a new
patient never requires retraining the model. This inductive procedure is how the system
would be deployed; it is \emph{not} the protocol of the reported experiments. Following the
released code of~\cite{belhadj2025plos}, the graph is built once over all the images of a
benchmark, and the test images are present in it as unlabelled nodes during training, a
transductive evaluation (\cref{sec:ch3-validity}). The inductive setting, with a graph built
from the training data only, is evaluated with DGTS in \cref{ch:dots}.

\subsection{Design Choices}
\label{sec:ch3-design}

\paragraph{An inductive architecture.}
Population-graph methods in medical imaging are often transductive: the test patients are
part of the graph during training~\cite{parisot2018disease}. This is convenient for a
research study but incompatible with a screening service, where new patients arrive every
day. GraphSAGE was chosen because it learns an aggregation \emph{function} rather than
node-specific embeddings, so that a new patient can be graded by connecting it to the
existing graph and applying the learned function (\cref{sec:sage}). The architecture is
therefore deployable inductively, although the experiments of this chapter, like those
of~\cite{parisot2018disease}, were run transductively.

\paragraph{Topology computed on vessel branch points.}
Persistent homology can be computed directly on the image intensities, using cubical
complexes, or on a point cloud extracted from the image. The first option captures every
bright or dark structure, including lesions, illumination gradients and noise. Computing
the Vietoris--Rips filtration on the branch points of the vessel skeleton restricts the
analysis to the vascular network and produces features that can be interpreted in
vascular terms (\cref{sec:tda-bio}). The price is a dependence on the quality of the
segmentation and thinning, which is mitigated by the pre-processing of stage S1 and by
the stability of persistence diagrams (\cref{eq:stability}).

\paragraph{A small vector of summary statistics.}
Richer representations of persistence diagrams exist, such as persistence landscapes or
images, and learned layers can process diagrams directly (\cref{sec:ph}). A
six-dimensional vector of summary statistics was preferred for two reasons: each component
has a direct interpretation, which matters for transparency, and the small dimension keeps
the computation of the topological similarity cheap. Its weight among the $1536$ dimensions
of the CNN embedding is small in the node features, which is why topology is also given an
explicit weight, $1-\alpha$, in the similarity that builds the graph.

\paragraph{Similarity fused at graph-construction time.}
TOPORET uses topology inside the edge weights (\cref{eq:dualsim}), so that two patients are
connected only if their images look alike \emph{and} their vessel networks have a similar
shape. Comparing the six summary statistics rather than the full diagrams keeps the
computation of all pairwise similarities tractable; a Wasserstein distance between
diagrams, which takes all their points into account, is a richer alternative that was not
evaluated. The balance $\alpha$ and the threshold $\tau$ were selected on validation data
rather than fixed a priori.

\paragraph{Exact pairwise graph construction.}
The dense $N\times N$ similarity matrix grows quadratically with the cohort, in time and in
memory, the main scalability limitation of TOPORET; a $k$-NN graph, as in DGTS
(\cref{ch:dots}), or approximate search~\cite{johnson2021faiss} removes it. Grading a new
patient requires only its $N$ similarities to the cohort (line~9 of \cref{alg:toporet}), a
cost linear in the size of the cohort.

\section{Experimentation}
\label{sec:ch3-exp}

\subsection{Datasets and Protocol}

TOPORET is evaluated on three public benchmarks: Kaggle DR~\cite{kaggle2015eyepacs} (the
$35{,}126$ images with public labels), Messidor-2~\cite{decenciere2014messidor} ($1{,}748$)
and APTOS~2019~\cite{aptos2019} ($3{,}662$). On each benchmark, a stratified five-fold
cross-validation (seed $42$) is run at the image level: each fold serves once as the test
set ($20\%$), and $12.5\%$ of the remaining images form the validation set, which gives a
$70/10/20$ partition per fold. Results are reported as mean $\pm$ standard deviation over
the five folds. The split is not grouped by patient, so the two eyes of a patient can fall
in different folds (\cref{sec:ch3-validity}). The CNN baseline is a two-layer perceptron
(hidden dimension $256$) on the same frozen EfficientNet-B3 features, trained with the same
loss, folds and schedule, without topology and without graph. Hyperparameters were
selected on the validation data only.

\subsection{Results on Three Benchmarks}
\label{sec:crossdata}

\Cref{tab:crossdata} reports the accuracy, QWK and macro-F1 of TOPORET and of the CNN
baseline on the three benchmarks. TOPORET improves every metric on every benchmark, by

largest on Messidor-2, whose standardised acquisition gives cleaner vessel skeletons, and

\subsection{Results: Contribution of the Population Graph (H1)}
\label{sec:h1}

\paragraph{H1 supported.}
On the primary benchmark, $t_4=3.82$ ($p=0.019$, two-tailed),
$d_z=1.71$ (very large effect), with a margin of $\times1.37$ over the pre-specified critical
value; the test is also passed on Messidor-2 and APTOS~2019. The population graph is
associated with a gain of $+0.04$ QWK and $+1.5$ percentage points of accuracy over
independent classification. Because the inferential unit is the cross-validation fold,
each paired $t$-test has four degrees of freedom.

\paragraph{Statistical robustness of H1.}
Five folds are a small sample for a paired $t$-test, even though each fold contains
thousands of images. Two supplementary analyses on the same five differences address this
concern: (i)~a bootstrap interval ($10{,}000$ resamples of the fold differences) for the QWK
gain, $95\%$ CI $[+0.021,\,+0.059]$, which excludes zero; (ii)~an exact sign-flip permutation
test over all $2^5=32$ sign assignments, which gives $p_{\mathrm{perm}}=1/32\approx0.031$, the
smallest value attainable with five folds, since the gain is positive in every fold. Both,
like the $t$-test, treat the folds as independent. Cross-validation folds, however, share
most of their training data, and the corrected resampled $t$-test of Nadeau and
Bengio~\cite{nadeau2003inference}, which accounts for this overlap, gives smaller
statistics: $t=2.45$ ($p=0.070$) on Kaggle DR, $2.63$ ($p=0.058$) on Messidor-2 and $2.25$
($p=0.088$) on APTOS~2019, each below the pre-specified threshold. The three benchmarks are
independent datasets, so their corrected $p$-values can be combined by Fisher's method,
which gives $p=0.015$. H1 is therefore supported by the pre-specified test and by the
combined corrected evidence of the three benchmarks, but not by the corrected test on any
single benchmark.

\paragraph{Where the gain comes from.}
\Cref{fig:toporetcm} compares the normalised confusion matrices of the CNN baseline and of
TOPORET on Kaggle DR~\cite{belhadj2025plos}. The diagonal is stronger for every grade, and
the largest improvement is at the Mild--Moderate boundary, the most ambiguous one for human
graders: Mild images predicted as Moderate drop from $6\%$ to $4\%$, and Moderate images
predicted as Mild from $6\%$ to $5\%$. No~DR reaches a per-class accuracy of $0.97$ and
proliferative DR $0.95$. The per-class F1 gains are largest for the rare and ambiguous
grades (Mild $+0.12$, Moderate and Severe $+0.09$), and the macro-average AUC rises from
$0.954$ to $0.970$. This is the behaviour expected from a population graph: a borderline
image is connected to similar patients, whose representations, learned from their grades, help to resolve the ambiguity.

\paragraph{H2 supported.}
Total $H_1$ persistence increases monotonically with severity, $F=84.3$, $p<0.001$, about
$23$ times the pre-specified critical value. Consistently, intra-grade
topological distances are systematically smaller than inter-grade distances for all
grades~\cite{belhadj2025plos}. The analysis demonstrates a statistically
significant association between the extracted topological descriptors and ICDR severity;
it does not show that topology causes, or mechanistically explains, disease severity, and
the $p$-value alone does not measure the size of the association. A one-way ANOVA was chosen
rather than a regression model because H2 was formulated as an association between one
predefined topological feature and the five ICDR grades, treated as groups; it makes no
assumption about the spacing between grades. Ordinal or regression models of the grade on
the feature, which would quantify the trend, are natural complementary analyses.

\subsection{Computational Cost}
\label{sec:toporet-cost}

The topological stage takes about $18$~ms per image on the CPU, and grading a new patient
takes about $23$~ms on an NVIDIA A100 GPU once its features are computed; training GraphSAGE
takes about eight minutes per benchmark on the GPU~\cite{belhadj2025plos}. The dense
similarity matrix of Kaggle DR has $35{,}126^2\approx1.2\times10^{9}$ entries (about
$5$~GB in single precision), so memory, rather than time, is the practical limit of the
exact graph.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:ch3-discussion}

\subsection{What the Graph and the Topology Each Contribute}
\label{sec:dualnecessary}

The ablation of \cref{tab:ablation3} separates three effects. The graph is the larger
contributor: with CNN-only node features, and topology present only in the edge
similarity, it improves QWK by $0.03$. Topological node features add a smaller gain on
their own ($+0.02$ QWK). Together, they give the largest gain ($+0.04$ QWK), close to the sum
of the individual contributions. The two axes are therefore complementary -- a result
reproduced on a second corpus by DGTS (\cref{ch:dots}) -- but there is no evidence of a
super-additive interaction. Because variant~(C) already uses topology in its edges, the
ablation contains no graph built from visual similarity alone, so the contribution of the
relational aggregation and that of the topological edge term are not separated here. DGTS,
whose graph is purely visual, isolates the relational effect ($+0.018$ QWK,
\cref{tab:dgts-abl}).

\subsection{Interpretation of the Topological Features}
\label{sec:tda-bio}

The six topological features have a direct vascular reading. Total $H_0$ persistence and
the number of long-lived components describe the fragmentation of the vessel network,
which increases with vascular rarefaction. Total $H_1$ persistence and the number of
long-lived loops describe closed vascular loops, which pathological neovascularisation
multiplies; the monotonic increase of $\Pi_{H_1}$ with severity (\cref{tab:anova}) is
consistent with this reading. Persistence entropy describes the diversity of the lifetimes
of topological features, a proxy for the diversity of vessel-segment lengths. These
mappings are post-hoc plausibility arguments; the statistical support for H2 rests on the
pre-specified ANOVA.

\subsection{From Supported Hypotheses to a Safety-Oriented System}
\label{sec:ch3-deploy}

H1 and H2 jointly support the view that the relational and structural axes contribute
useful information to grading. They do not, however, address safety. TOPORET grades every
image, does not model the order of the grades, and was evaluated with accuracy-type
metrics only, on each benchmark separately. A $+0.04$ QWK gain and a significant ANOVA
are what a methods paper reports as success, but neither shows how many severe cases a
system would leave unreferred. \Cref{ch:dots} is organised around closing this gap,
together with two further gaps (single-source training and the grade-agnostic loss).

\paragraph{Scope of H2: statistical association, not clinical validation.}
The ANOVA establishes that $\Pi_{H_1}$ is statistically associated with ICDR grade. Three
caveats bound this claim.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Association is not independent predictive value.} The ANOVA does not show
that TDA features add predictive value beyond visual features. The ablation of
\cref{tab:ablation3} provides this separately: TDA features add $+0.02$ QWK to the visual
features.
\item \textbf{Single-dataset test.} H2 is tested on Kaggle DR only. Cross-dataset stability
of the TDA feature distributions is supported by the stability theorem
(\cref{eq:stability}) but was not validated by an external ANOVA.
\item \textbf{Not a clinically validated biomarker.} Clinical validation would require
prospective studies, regulatory-grade reproducibility protocols and independent clinical
reading. The term used throughout this thesis is therefore \emph{severity-associated
topological feature}, not \emph{biomarker}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:ch3-validity}

\paragraph{Internal validity.}
The baseline and TOPORET share the same frozen EfficientNet-B3 features and the same
folds, and both are two-layer networks with the same width, loss and schedule; they differ
in the graph aggregation of GraphSAGE and in the topology. The evaluation is
transductive: the test images are unlabelled nodes of the graph during training, so their
features, but not their grades, influence the representations of their neighbours. The
reported figures are those of this setting; an inductive evaluation of the same model was
not run, and the inductive setting is evaluated with DGTS (\cref{ch:dots}). Hyperparameters were selected
on validation data only, and the decision thresholds of the tests were fixed before the
experiments.

\paragraph{External validity.}
Each benchmark is evaluated within itself; the results show that the gain is reproduced on
three datasets from three continents, not that a model trained on one transfers to another
without retraining. The folds are drawn at the image level. On Kaggle DR and Messidor-2,
which contain both eyes of each patient, a test image can therefore be connected in the
graph to the fellow eye of the same patient, whose grade is usually the same, and part of
the gain attributed to the graph may come from this fellow-eye information, which a
patient-level split would remove. The gain is also observed on APTOS~2019, which provides
one image per patient~\cite{belhadj2026dgts}, where this effect cannot occur. No dataset
provides demographic information.

\paragraph{Construct validity.}
QWK measures agreement with the reference grade, which itself contains grader noise
(\cref{sec:grading}); a part of the remaining disagreement may therefore reflect label
errors rather than model errors. The topological features depend on the quality of the
vessel segmentation, so a failure of stage S1 would appear as a change of topology.

\section*{Conclusion}
\phantomsection\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Conclusion}

TOPORET~\cite{belhadj2025plos} improves on a strong CNN baseline on three benchmarks
($+1.5$ to $+2.3$ percentage points of accuracy, $+0.04$ to $+0.07$ QWK). Its pre-specified
tests supported H1 ($t_4=3.82$, $p=0.019$ two-tailed, $d_z=1.71$; $p=0.015$ for the three
benchmarks combined after correction for fold overlap) and H2 as a statistical association
($F=84.3$, $p<0.001$); the graph is the larger contributor, the topology a complementary
one. These results were obtained transductively, with image-level folds. TOPORET, however, is trained on one dataset at a time, ignores the
order of the grades and does not measure missed referrals; the next chapter addresses
these gaps with DGTS and DOTS.
